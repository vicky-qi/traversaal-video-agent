## Q6. What was the average electric car battery pack price per kWh in 2015 vs. the latest full year, and what share of a new electric car's price does the battery make up today?

- **Q6-F1** In 2015, BloombergNEF's market survey put the price of a plug-in car battery pack at about $399 per kWh (2015, US national estimate, plug-in hybrid packs), down from $615 per kWh in 2014.
  - Source: [U.S. DOE EERE, "Cost and Price Metrics for Automotive Lithium-Ion Batteries" (fact sheet)](https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2017/06/f34/67089_EERE_LIB_cost_vs_price_metrics_r9_0.pdf) · official · published 2017-02 · scope: US
  - Quote: "Estimated national prices and costs of light-duty plug-in hybrid electric vehicle cells and packs for 2014 and 2015 from several sources."
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE; CONFLICT with Q6-F2 [The $399 (2015) and $615 (2014) values are the BNEF "Market Price" bar labels in Figure 1, pack including cells. Underlying source: BNEF "H2 2015 Electric Vehicle Battery Price Survey" (Curry 2015), as cited by DOE. The same figure has other 2015 numbers that measure different things and are not conflicts: DOE's "lab achieved cost" of $264/kWh, which is a projected cost for technology still in R&D, and modeled costs to manufacture of $492/kWh (BNEF) and $495/kWh (CEMAC).]
  - Check: VERIFIED (DOE fact sheet; WebFetch gave no text, so I read the saved PDF. Figure 1's "Market Price" section labels the BNEF pack bars $615 (2014) and $399 (2015), with the cell share inside them at $367 and $234. The caption is the quoted sentence and does not itself show the numbers. The text calls market prices "market data surveys". Curry (2015), BNEF's "H2 2015 Electric Vehicle Battery Price Survey", is one of the figure's listed sources, but the figure does not link each bar to a reference. Paraphrased words: "plug-in car" for "plug-in hybrid electric vehicle", and "US" for the caption's "national" (this is a US DOE document). No dollar year is given. WARN: these are plug-in hybrid packs, which cost more per kWh than battery-electric packs. Setting $399 against 2025's $99 battery-electric figure (Q6-F5) mixes two pack types, so on screen say "plug-in hybrid packs".)

- **Q6-F2** In 2015, a second market survey, by Total Battery Consulting, put plug-in hybrid battery pack prices higher, at about $477 per kWh (2015, US national estimate), down from $538 per kWh in 2014.
  - Source: [U.S. DOE EERE, "Cost and Price Metrics for Automotive Lithium-Ion Batteries" (fact sheet)](https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2017/06/f34/67089_EERE_LIB_cost_vs_price_metrics_r9_0.pdf) · official · published 2017-02 · scope: US
  - Quote: "First, the market price data show a significant price drop between 2014 and 2015 (11% to 35%, depending on source)."
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE; CONFLICT with Q6-F1 [The $477 (2015) and $538 (2014) values are the Total Battery Consulting "Market Price" bar labels in Figure 1. These match the quoted 11% drop, while BNEF's $615 to $399 matches the 35% drop. The two surveys differ by about 20% for the same year. DOE says these gaps come partly from how "cost" and "price" are defined.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (DOE fact sheet, read from the saved PDF. Figure 1's "Market Price" section labels the Total Battery Consulting pack bars $538 (2014) and $477 (2015). The reference list gives Anderman (2016), Total Battery Consulting's "xEV Industry Insider Report", a market research report. The quoted sentence is on page 2 and matches word for word, but it does not show the $477 figure itself. My arithmetic: $538 to $477 is an 11% drop and $615 to $399 is a 35% drop, which matches "11% to 35%". WARN: these are plug-in hybrid packs, as in Q6-F1.)

- **Q6-F3** In 2023, an electric car battery pack cost about $140 per kWh, roughly 90% less than around 2008–2010, when it was about $1,400 per kWh.
  - Source: [U.S. DOE Vehicle Technologies Office, FOTW #1354](https://www.energy.gov/node/4843419) · official · published 2024-08 · scope: US
  - Quote: "The 2023 estimate is $139/kWh on a usable-energy basis"
  - Source 2: [IEA, Batteries and Secure Energy Transitions – Executive summary](https://www.iea.org/reports/batteries-and-secure-energy-transitions/executive-summary) · international · published 2024 (month not shown) · scope: global
  - Quote 2: "from USD 1 400 per kilowatt-hour in 2010" … "to less than USD 140 per kilowatt-hour in 2023"
  - Flags: none [The two measure slightly different things, so this is not a conflict. DOE's figure is a modeled cost to make the pack, on usable energy, at a scale of at least 100,000 packs a year, in 2023 dollars, compared with $1,415/kWh in 2008 ("90% lower"). IEA's figure is a battery price, compared with 2010. They agree on about $140/kWh in 2023 and a drop of about 90%.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (Source 1, DOE FOTW #1354, dated 2024-08-05. It says "$139/kWh on a usable-energy basis for production at scale of at least 100,000 units per year", "$1,415/kWh in 2008", and that cost "declined 90% between 2008 and 2023 (using 2023 constant dollars)". The page title says "Battery Pack Costs for a Light-Duty Vehicle". Paraphrased words: "electric car" for "light-duty vehicle". WARN: DOE's baseline year is 2008 only. The 2010 end of "around 2008–2010" comes from Source 2 (IEA), which covers lithium-ion battery prices in general ("both EV and storage applications"), says "less than USD 140" and puts the "90% cost reductions" "since 2010". DOE's figure is a modeled cost to make a pack, not a sale price. In the script, say "cost to make" and "2008".)

- **Q6-F4** In 2025, the average price of a lithium-ion battery pack across all uses fell 8% from 2024 to a record low of $108 per kWh (2025, global).
  - Source: [BloombergNEF press release, "Lithium-Ion Battery Pack Prices Fall to $108 per Kilowatt-Hour…"](https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-energy/lithium-ion-battery-pack-prices-fall-to-108-per-kilowatt-hour) · industry · published 2025-12 · scope: global
  - Quote: "lithium-ion battery pack prices have dropped 8% since 2024 to a record low of $108 per kilowatt-hour."
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [IEA's [Global EV Outlook 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2026/electric-vehicle-batteries) also reports "Average battery prices declined by 8% in 2025". IEA's battery price figures are based on BNEF's survey, so it is not independent. The [GEVO 2025 batteries page](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2025/electric-vehicle-batteries) says "IEA analysis based on data from Bloomberg and Bloomberg New Energy Finance Lithium-Ion Price Survey". The press release does not say whether the dollars are real or nominal. The $108 average covers all uses, including stationary storage.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (BNEF press release, dated 2025-12-09; the quote matches. The release says the survey "covers multiple battery end-uses", including types of EVs and stationary storage, and gives segment and regional prices separately, so $108 is the survey's overall headline figure. The words "average", "across all uses" and "global" are not printed next to $108; they are a fair plain reading of that headline figure. WARN: $108 includes stationary storage and other non-car uses. For an electric car pack, use $99 (Q6-F5). The release does not say whether the dollars are real or nominal.)

- **Q6-F5** In 2025, packs for battery-electric cars were the cheapest type in transport, at an average of $99 per kWh (2025, global).
  - Source: [BloombergNEF press release, "Lithium-Ion Battery Pack Prices Fall to $108 per Kilowatt-Hour…"](https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-energy/lithium-ion-battery-pack-prices-fall-to-108-per-kilowatt-hour) · industry · published 2025-12 · scope: global
  - Quote: "Battery electric vehicles (BEVs) packs were the cheapest in the transport segment at $99/kWh."
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [WebFetch's summary says the release calls this the second year below $100/kWh; that wording was not captured as a quote, so check it. This is the figure closest to "an electric car battery pack price" for 2025.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (BNEF press release; the quote matches. The flag's open point is confirmed: the same sentence goes on "the second year that they were below the $100/kWh threshold." Paraphrased words: "cars" for "vehicles (BEVs)". The release does not use "average" for the $99, but it is the survey's price for that segment.)

- **Q6-F6** Battery prices depend a lot on the region. In 2025, average pack prices were lowest in China at $84 per kWh, while North America was about 44% higher and Europe about 56% higher (2025).
  - Source: [BloombergNEF press release, "Lithium-Ion Battery Pack Prices Fall to $108 per Kilowatt-Hour…"](https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-energy/lithium-ion-battery-pack-prices-fall-to-108-per-kilowatt-hour) · industry · published 2025-12 · scope: China, North America, Europe
  - Quote: "Average battery pack prices were lowest in China, at $84/kWh." (The release gives North America and Europe as "44% and 56% higher".)
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [IEA [Global EV Outlook 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2026/electric-vehicle-batteries) gives a consistent picture: "In 2025, battery pack prices in China were 30% lower than in North America, and 35% lower than in Europe". It is probably based on BNEF data, so it is not counted as independent. See Gaps for the consistency check.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (BNEF press release. The paragraph opens "The report also covers regional differences in pricing", then gives the quoted sentence, then "Pack prices in the North America and Europe were 44% and 56% higher, reflecting higher local production costs". From the context, the comparison is with China. Paraphrased words: "depend a lot on the region" for "regional differences in pricing". The release gives exactly 44% and 56%, so "about" is a harmless addition.)

- **Q6-F7** In its January 2025 analysis, the US Department of Energy estimated batteries for US passenger cars, SUVs and pickups at $128–133 per kWh (2024 dollars), down from the $150 per kWh it used in 2022.
  - Source: [U.S. DOE Vehicle Technologies Office, "2025 Incremental Purchase Cost Methodology and Results for Clean Vehicles"](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2025-01/2025.01.13_DOE_Incremental_Cost_Report_for_publication.pdf) · official · published 2025-01 · scope: US
  - Quote: "Battery costs for light-duty vehicles … were captured as $128-133/kWh, reduced from $150/kWh used in the 2022 analysis"
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [This is DOE's estimate of what US carmakers can currently pay for a battery, citing an Argonne National Laboratory cost study of US-made batteries. It is not a global survey price. It differs from BNEF's global $99–$108/kWh because it measures something different (US cost, mostly 2024 conditions), not because the two conflict.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (DOE report, dated January 2025; WebFetch gave no text, so I read the saved PDF. The Executive Summary reads "Battery costs for light-duty vehicles, sport utility vehicles, pick-up trucks and Class 3 vans were captured as $128-133/kWh, reduced from $150/kWh used in the 2022 analysis". Footnote 9 cites Argonne's "Cost Analysis and Projections for U.S.-Manufactured Automotive Lithium-ion Batteries" (2024-01-01). Paraphrased words: "passenger cars" for "light-duty vehicles"; the claim leaves out Class 3 vans. No dollar year is printed next to the $/kWh range. The report's battery tables are in 2024$, and by my arithmetic $9,185 ÷ 72 kWh ≈ $128/kWh, which is consistent. The page calls these "an average price that DOE estimates OEMs can currently achieve for batteries".)

- **Q6-F8** DOE's model of a compact battery-electric car (72 kWh battery, 300-mile range) puts the battery at $9,185 and the whole car at a purchase cost of $33,690 (2024 dollars, US). For a midsize electric SUV, the figures are $11,032 for the battery and $41,329 for the car.
  - Source: [U.S. DOE Vehicle Technologies Office, "2025 Incremental Purchase Cost Methodology and Results for Clean Vehicles"](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2025-01/2025.01.13_DOE_Incremental_Cost_Report_for_publication.pdf) · official · published 2025-01 · scope: US
  - Quote: "2025 Report BEV High Voltage Battery Cost … Compact Car $9,185" / "Modeled Representative Vehicle Purchase Cost, 2025 (2024$) … Compact Car $25,742 $33,690"
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [In Table 5, $25,742 is the gas car and $33,690 the battery-electric car. Battery size and range come from Table A-1, "Compact Car 72 / 300". The vehicle purchase costs include a retail markup: "This analysis assumes a RPE of 1.5 for LDVs … to approximate retail prices". The battery figures are costs to the carmaker. Midsize car: battery $9,929, car $36,394. Pickup: battery $15,368, car $51,244. The battery's share of the price is my own calculation, in Gaps.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (DOE report, read from the saved PDF. The 2024$ BEV high-voltage battery cost is $9,185 for the compact car and $11,032 for the midsize SUV (Table ES-1 and Table 6). Table 5, "Modeled Representative Vehicle Purchase Cost, 2025 (2024$)", gives BEV compact $33,690, BEV midsize SUV $41,329 and Conv. compact $25,742. Table A-1 gives the compact BEV as "72 / 300". The flag's other figures and the RPE 1.5 sentence also match. WARN: the car figure includes the 1.5 retail price markup, while the battery figure is a cost to the carmaker. Both are for modeled "representative" vehicles (the compact class example is the Honda Civic), not real EV sticker prices. Any "share of the price" the script states must say how it was worked out.)

- **Q6-F9** The battery is the main reason an electric car costs more to build than a comparable gas car. DOE estimates that a compact battery-electric car costs about $7,900 more to buy than a comparable gas car (2024 dollars, US).
  - Source: [U.S. DOE Vehicle Technologies Office, "2025 Incremental Purchase Cost Methodology and Results for Clean Vehicles"](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2025-01/2025.01.13_DOE_Incremental_Cost_Report_for_publication.pdf) · official · published 2025-01 · scope: US
  - Quote: "For BEVs and PHEVs, battery costs comprise the majority of the incremental manufacturing cost difference"
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [The $7,900 is from Table ES-2, "BEV – ICE (2024$) … Compact Car $7,900". The same table gives $9,600 for a midsize car, $11,000 for a midsize SUV and $18,800 for a pickup. DOE models the powertrain only and leaves out incentives, discounts and tax credits.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (DOE report, Results section: "For BEVs and PHEVs, battery costs comprise the majority of the incremental manufacturing cost difference and thus are the primary determinant in modeling incremental purchase cost". Table ES-2, BEV – ICE (2024$), gives $7,900 for the compact car, and the flag's other figures match. Paraphrased words: "main reason" for "majority … primary determinant", and "costs … more to buy" for "incremental purchase cost". WARN: this is a modeled, powertrain-only difference that leaves out "incentives, discounts, tax credits". It is not a gap between real showroom prices.)

- **Q6-F10** One industry estimate shows the battery's share of a car's price shrinking: the lithium-ion cells in a Tesla Model 3 Base fell from 15% of the car's retail price to 7.5% by August 2024.
  - Source: [Fastmarkets, "Electric vehicle economics: How lithium-ion cell costs impact EV prices"](https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/electric-vehicle-economics-how-lithium-ion-battery-costs-impact-ev-prices/) · industry · published 2024-11 · scope: one model (Tesla Model 3 Base); market not stated in the text returned
  - Quote: "the cost of Li-ion cells inside the Tesla Model 3 Base as a % of its retail price fell from 15% to 7.5%"
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [This covers cells only, not the full pack with its casing, wiring, cooling and electronics, and only one low-cost LFP model. It measures something different from the DOE pack figures in Q6-F8, so it is not a conflict. The start date of the period is unclear in the processed text (January 2022 or start of 2023), so check it on the page.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (Fastmarkets, by Muthu Krishna, dated 2024-11-05. The sentence reads "Between this period, the cost of Li-ion cells inside the Tesla Model 3 Base as a % of its retail price fell from 15% to 7.5%", and the page gives 7.5% "as of August 2024". The start date is still unclear on the page itself: the body says the 15% was at the start of 2023, but the key takeaways say "Jan 22". This fetch reports that the chart covers the US Model 3, that the cell cost counts materials and manufacturing but not taxes or margins, and that Tesla has since stopped selling the Model 3 Base. WARN: the figure covers cells only, not the whole battery pack. The lead-in "the battery's share" should be said as "the cost of the cells". It is one LFP model and Fastmarkets' own analysis.)
