## Q4. For the best-selling electric cars of the latest model year, what are the battery size (kWh), rated range and fastest charging time (for example, 10–80%), and how much do cold and hot weather change range and charging speed?

- **Q4-F1** In the US in 2025, the Tesla Model Y was the top-selling electric car with 357,528 sold, the Chevrolet Equinox EV sold 57,945 and the Hyundai Ioniq 5 sold 47,039 (Kelley Blue Book figures, full year 2025).
  - Source: [Electrek: Top 10 best-selling EVs in the US in 2025](https://electrek.co/2026/01/13/top-10-best-selling-evs-us-2025/) · news · published 2026-01 · scope: US
  - Quote: "Top 10 best-selling EVs in the US in 2025 (Source: Official sales data/ Kelley Blue Book)" … "Tesla Model Y 357,528 -4.0%"
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [Electrek reports Kelley Blue Book (Cox Automotive) estimates. Cox's own page, [Cox Automotive Q4 2025 EV sales](https://www.coxautoinc.com/market-insights/q4-2025-ev-sales/), gives brand totals only: "Tesla sales fell to 589,000 in 2025, down 7% from 2024". It is the same data owner, so it is not independent.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (Electrek, dated 2026-01-13. Its table "Top 10 best-selling EVs in the US in 2025" has a "2025 sales" column and lists Tesla Model Y first at 357,528, Chevrolet Equinox EV at 57,945 and Hyundai IONIQ 5 at 47,039; all three numbers match exactly. Paraphrased: "top-selling" (the article never says this in prose; the Model Y is the table's first and largest row), "full year 2025" (the page says "2025 sales" and "last year") and "Kelley Blue Book figures" (the caption says "Official sales data/ Kelley Blue Book"). WARN: the caption mixes official data with KBB estimates and does not say which rows are estimates. A section heading also reads "outside of Tesla" even though the table includes Tesla. Present the sales figures as "about" or "an estimated".)

- **Q4-F2** The 2026 Tesla Model Y Long Range has a battery of about 81 kWh. It is EPA-rated at 357 miles with rear-wheel drive and 327 miles with all-wheel drive, and the cheaper Standard RWD (18-inch wheels) is rated 321 miles (model year 2026).
  - Source: [fueleconomy.gov: 2026 Tesla Model Y](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/PowerSearch.do?action=noform&path=1&year1=2026&year2=2026&make=Tesla&baseModel=Model%20Y&srchtyp=ymm) · official · undated (2026 model-year listing) · scope: US
  - Quote: "2026 Tesla Model Y Long Range RWD" … "357 miles" … "Total Range"
  - Source 2: [Kelley Blue Book: 2026 Tesla Model Y Review](https://www.kbb.com/tesla/model-y/2026/) · consumer guide · updated 2025-12 · scope: US
  - Quote 2: "Battery Capacity" … "Long Range" … "81 kWh" (table row, cut off)
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [The range figures are the official EPA ratings, which other sites copy. Tesla does not publish the battery size: KBB gives 81 kWh, and EV Database (see Q4-F3) lists a near match for the related European Premium AWD of "Nominal Capacity * 82.0 kWh" and 79.0 kWh usable. KBB's own range column is muddled (it shows 327 miles for several trims), so use the EPA figures.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (The two sources together. Source 1, fueleconomy.gov, gives the 2026 ratings: Long Range RWD 357 miles, Long Range AWD 327 miles, Standard RWD (18in Wheels) 321 miles. It lists the Standard RWD at $39,990, against $44,990 and $48,990 for the Long Range versions, which supports "cheaper". It shows no battery size. Source 2, KBB, updated 2025-12-16, lists 81 kWh for the Long Range trims in its Styles table. WARN: 321 miles applies only to 18-inch wheels; the 19-inch Standard RWD is rated 303 miles. KBB's own range figures (a 294–327 mile band and 321 miles in its specs) do not match the EPA trims, so use only the EPA numbers. The 81 kWh is KBB's figure, not Tesla's.)

  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [This is EV Database's estimate for the European model. Tesla's US site returned 403 twice, so I have no US carmaker figure.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (EV Database lists "Tesla Model Y Premium AWD (Juniper) (2026)", available since August 2026, under its European Overview. In its "Fast Charging (10 -> 80%)" table, the "Supercharger v3 (250 kW DC)" row gives 28 minutes, and the table key defines time as 10% to 80%. "Charge Power (max)" is 250 kW DC. WARN: this is an EV Database estimate for a European Premium AWD. It is not the US Long Range RWD whose 357-mile range is in Q4-F2, and it is not a Tesla figure: the page's Tesla-claimed table shows no charge time. Present it as an independent estimate for a European version.)
