# Video Brief: Snow White as the Brothers Grimm Wrote It

**Assumptions:** The prompt asks for a retelling, not an argument, so the film retells the Grimm version of the tale and quietly sets it against the version most viewers already know (the softer modern film retelling), because that contrast is what makes a two-minute retelling worth watching. "As the Brothers Grimm wrote it" is taken to mean the published Grimm text; the researcher should say which edition the film follows (first edition or the final revised one) and flag any plot points that changed between editions. Every plot detail mentioned below (mirror, forest, dwarfs, attempts on her life, glass coffin, ending) is a working guess for the story shape only and must be confirmed against the Grimm text before it reaches the script. Violent elements of the original will be told plainly but not shown graphically, to suit a general audience. Characters are drawn as paper-cut silhouettes, not portraits of real people.

- **Original prompt:** "Tell the story of Snow White as the Brothers Grimm wrote it, for a general audience"
- **Audience:** General adult viewers who know the fairy tale mostly from children's books or the animated film, and have never read the Grimm text.
- **Goal:** The viewer can retell the Grimm plot from start to finish and name the main ways it differs from the version they grew up with.
- **Angle:** The Snow White the Grimms wrote down is a darker, stranger story than the one most of us remember, and following it step by step shows what was smoothed away.
- **Tone:** Storyteller's voice: warm, a little eerie, clear and never gory.
- **Length:** 2 minutes, about 288 words of narration

## Story spine

- **Opening moment:** A closed, worn storybook sits in darkness; its cover creaks open and a paper-cut castle pops up, with a mirror on the wall that begins to glow.
- **The question:** What actually happens in Snow White when you read the Grimms' own words, and how different is it from the story we think we know?
- **Act 1 (set-up):** The viewer meets the queen, the mirror and the girl, and learns that the film will follow the Grimm text rather than the familiar version, so they should watch for what is different.
- **Act 2 (journey):** The story moves page by page: the queen's jealousy drives Snow White into the forest, she finds shelter with the dwarfs, and the queen keeps coming back in disguise until the girl falls into a death-like sleep. Each step raises the next question: how did she survive, and how will it end?
- **Act 3 (payoff):** The ending as the Grimms wrote it, including what happens to the queen, is told and then set beside the familiar version; the storybook closes on the mirror, now dark.

## Art direction

- **Style:** Layered paper-cut storybook: flat silhouette shapes on stacked paper layers with soft drop shadows, gentle parallax as the camera drifts between layers, and a warm glow for magic.
- **Palette:** #14121F (night background), #F2EAD8 (paper white), #B3202E (apple red), #2E5E4E (forest green), #C9A44C (mirror gold), #6E6A86 (dusk shadow)
- **Mood:** Hushed and curious, darkening through the middle, landing on a quiet, slightly chilling close.
- **Music mood:** tense
- **Hero visual:** An open pop-up storybook whose pages turn to reveal each paper-cut set (castle, forest, cottage, coffin), with a gold oval mirror that reappears on every spread and glows whenever the queen acts. All of it is shapes, gradients and silhouettes drawn in code.

## Key questions for research

1. In the Grimms' published text of "Little Snow White" (Sneewittchen), what is the plot in order, scene by scene, and which edition is the standard source (first edition versus the final revised edition)?
2. Who is the antagonist in the Grimm text: is she Snow White's mother or stepmother, and did this change between the Grimm editions?
3. What exactly does the queen order the huntsman to do and to bring back, and how does Snow White escape?
4. How many times does the queen try to kill Snow White, what disguise and object does she use each time, and how is Snow White saved after each attempt?
5. How does Snow White wake in the Grimm text (what causes the poisoned piece to come loose), and what happens to the queen at the wedding?
6. Which of these Grimm details are changed or left out in the best-known modern film adaptation, according to reputable folklore scholarship or major publications?

## Out of scope

- Other fairy tales, and other Grimm stories beyond Snow White.
- Scholarly debates on the tale's psychological or feminist readings, beyond a single line of contrast.
- The history of the Brothers Grimm themselves, their collecting methods and their sources.
- Detailed production history or box-office figures of any film adaptation.
- Claims about a "real historical Snow White."

## Notes for the researcher

- Best sources: an English translation of the Grimm text from a university or library collection (for example D. L. Ashliman's folktexts at the University of Pittsburgh, or Project Gutenberg editions of the tales), the German text, and folklore scholarship that compares the editions; for the comparison with the familiar film, use reputable encyclopedias or major news and arts publications.
- Quote plot points directly from the text where possible and name the edition and translation used, because the script retells the story closely.
- Flag clearly any detail that differs between the first and final Grimm editions, so the script follows one edition consistently.
- No figures need to be recent; this is a fixed historical text. Only the comparison with the modern adaptation needs a reliable published source.
