## Q5. How does Snow White wake in the Grimm text (what causes the poisoned piece to come loose), and what happens to the queen at the wedding?

**Short answer:** In the final Grimm edition (1857, the 7th edition, which is the standard text), a prince talks the dwarfs into giving him the glass coffin. As his servants carry it away on their shoulders, they stumble over a shrub, the jolt shakes the bit of poisoned apple out of Snow White's throat, and she sits up alive. The stepmother is invited to the wedding, sees Snow White there, and is made to put on iron shoes heated red-hot in the coals and dance until she falls dead. **Choice made:** this file follows the 1857 final edition (D. L. Ashliman's English translation, checked against the German text on Wikisource), because that is the version almost all modern readers and translations use. The 1812 first edition differs on both points, flagged in Q5-F9 and Q5-F10: an angry servant hits her on the back to wake her, and the woman at the wedding is her own mother.

**Worth showing:** the glass coffin lifted onto servants' shoulders, tilting as they stumble over a shrub, and the apple piece flying loose; Snow White lifting the lid and sitting up; the gold mirror glowing one last time to call the "young queen" a thousand times fairer; the queen frozen at the wedding door; iron shoes glowing in the coals, lifted out with tongs, then a frantic dance that ends in a fall. (1812 alternative: a servant lifting her upright and striking her back.)

## Gaps

- Ashliman's 1857 page gives the place of publication as "Berlin, 1857"; German Wikisource lists the 1857 edition as Dieterich, Göttingen. I did not settle this. The script does not need the city; the fact-checker may want to look into it.
- The 1812 striking scene comes from Ashliman's note and translation plus the German 1812 text. I did not open a peer-reviewed study comparing the editions. Search results pointed to Jack Zipes's translation of the first edition (*The Original Folk and Fairy Tales of the Brothers Grimm*, 2014), but I did not open it.
- Project Gutenberg's English editions (Taylor/Edwardes, ebook 2591; Margaret Hunt, ebook 5314) were too long for WebFetch to reach tale 53, so I did not use them.
- UNVERIFIED: the earlier 1810 manuscript reportedly has Snow White's father's physicians reviving her. This appeared only in a search snippet and I did not confirm it on an opened page. It is also outside the brief's published-text scope.
