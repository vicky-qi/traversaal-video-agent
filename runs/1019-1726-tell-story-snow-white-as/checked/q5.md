## Q5. How does Snow White wake in the Grimm text (what causes the poisoned piece to come loose), and what happens to the queen at the wedding?

- **Q5-F2** In the final edition a prince finds the coffin on the mountain, asks the dwarfs for it ("I will give you anything you want for it"), and the dwarfs pity him and hand it over.
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White (Ashliman)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · undated · scope: Germany (text, 1857 ed.)
  - Quote: "Let me have the coffin. I will give you anything you want for it." / "the good dwarfs felt pity for him and gave him the coffin."
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [plot detail from the translation only; no number involved]
  - Check: VERIFIED (Ashliman; both quotes on the page verbatim, plus "He saw the coffin on the mountain with beautiful Snow-White in it". Paraphrase: "hand it over" for "gave him the coffin".)

- **Q5-F3** In the final edition the prince's servants carry the coffin away on their shoulders, they stumble over a shrub, and the jolt knocks the piece of poisoned apple out of Snow White's throat.
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White (Ashliman)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · undated · scope: Germany (text, 1857 ed.)
  - Quote: "one of them stumbled on some brush, and this dislodged from Snow-White's throat the piece of poisoned apple"
  - Source 2: [Sneewittchen (1857), Wikisource (German)](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Sneewittchen_(1857)) · data service · undated · scope: Germany (text)
  - Quote 2: "Da geschah es, daß sie über einen Strauch stolperten, und von dem Schüttern fuhr der giftige Apfelgrütz, den Sneewittchen abgebissen hatte, aus dem Hals."
  - Flags: none [Small wording difference: the German says "they" (the servants) stumbled; Ashliman says "one of them". Safest script wording: "the servants stumbled". Ashliman's "brush" and the German "Strauch" (shrub) mean the same thing. The servants carrying it on their shoulders: "Der Königssohn ließ ihn nun von seinen Dienern auf den Schultern forttragen."]
  - Check: VERIFIED (Wikisource 1857 German text fully supports "they stumbled" ("sie ... stolperten"), "shrub" (Strauch), "jolt" (Schüttern) and shoulders ("auf den Schultern forttragen"). Ashliman also confirms shoulders: "The prince had his servants carry it away on their shoulders." WARN: Ashliman's English says only one servant stumbled; if the script quotes Ashliman, say "one of them stumbled", not "the servants".)

- **Q5-F4** Snow White then opens her eyes, lifts the lid of the coffin, sits up alive, and cries out, "Good heavens, where am I?"
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White (Ashliman)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · undated · scope: Germany (text, 1857 ed.)
  - Quote: "Not long afterward she opened her eyes, lifted the lid from her coffin, sat up, and was alive again." / "Good heavens, where am I?"
  - Source 2: [Sneewittchen (1857), Wikisource (German)](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Sneewittchen_(1857)) · data service · undated · scope: Germany (text)
  - Quote 2: "„Ach Gott, wo bin ich?“ rief es."
  - Flags: none
  - Check: VERIFIED (Ashliman, both quotes verbatim; German cry confirmed on Wikisource.)

- **Q5-F5** The prince tells her he loves her more than anything in the world and asks her to come to his father's castle and be his wife, and a splendid wedding is planned.
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White (Ashliman)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · undated · scope: Germany (text, 1857 ed.)
  - Quote: "I love you more than anything else in the world. Come with me to my father's castle. You shall become my wife."
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [plot detail; also "Their wedding was planned with great splendor and majesty."]
  - Check: VERIFIED (Ashliman; both lines verbatim. Paraphrase: "splendid" for "with great splendor and majesty".)

- **Q5-F6** The "godless" stepmother is invited to the wedding feast, and when she asks the mirror who is fairest, it answers that the young queen is "a thousand times fairer" than she is.
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White (Ashliman)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · undated · scope: Germany (text, 1857 ed.)
  - Quote: "Snow-White's godless stepmother was also invited to the feast." / "But the young queen is a thousand times fairer than you."
  - Source 2: [Sneewittchen (1857), Wikisource (German)](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Sneewittchen_(1857)) · data service · undated · scope: Germany (text)
  - Quote 2: "Zu dem Fest wurde aber auch Sneewittchens gottlose Stiefmutter eingeladen." / "aber die junge Königin ist tausendmal schöner als ihr."
  - Flags: none ["a thousand times" is the mirror's wording, not a measured figure.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (both sources verbatim. The full mirror answer begins "You, my queen, are fair; it is true." WARN: she asks the mirror before she goes to the wedding, at home; do not picture the mirror at the wedding itself.)

- **Q5-F7** When she arrives at the wedding she recognizes Snow White and stands frozen with fear, unable to move.
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White (Ashliman)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · undated · scope: Germany (text, 1857 ed.)
  - Quote: "When she arrived she recognized Snow-White, and terrorized, she could only stand there without moving."
  - Source 2: [Sneewittchen (1857), Wikisource (German)](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Sneewittchen_(1857)) · data service · undated · scope: Germany (text)
  - Quote 2: "vor Angst und Schrecken stand sie da und konnte sich nicht regen."
  - Flags: none
  - Check: VERIFIED (both sources; German also has "erkannte sie Sneewittchen". Paraphrase: "frozen with fear" for "terrorized ... without moving".)

- **Q5-F8** Her punishment: iron shoes have been heated in burning coals and are brought in with tongs, and she is forced to step into the red-hot shoes and dance until she falls down dead.
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White (Ashliman)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · undated · scope: Germany (text, 1857 ed.)
  - Quote: "They were brought forth with tongs and placed before her." / "She was forced to step into the red-hot shoes and dance until she fell down dead."
  - Source 2: [Sneewittchen (1857), Wikisource (German)](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Sneewittchen_(1857)) · data service · undated · scope: Germany (text)
  - Quote 2: "Aber es waren schon eiserne Pantoffeln über Kohlenfeuer gestellt" / "Da mußte sie in die rothglühenden Schuhe treten und so lange tanzen, bis sie todt zur Erde fiel."
  - Flags: none [Brief asks for this to be told plainly, not shown graphically.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (both sources; Ashliman also has "Then they put a pair of iron shoes into burning coals." German has "mit Zangen herein getragen".)

- **Q5-F9** EDITION DIFFERENCE: in the 1812 first edition the prince has the coffin carried into his castle and the servants who must haul it around for him grow angry, until one lifts Snow White upright and hits her on the back, which knocks the apple piece out of her throat. From 1819 on, the servant stumbling replaces this.
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White (Ashliman), notes](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · undated · scope: Germany (text)
  - Quote: "In the first edition the apple is dislodged when a servant, angry for having to carry Snow-White's coffin ... strikes the sleeping princess." (cut off)
  - Source 2: [Sneewittchen (Schneeweißchen) (1812), Wikisource (German)](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Sneewittchen_(Schneewei%C3%9Fchen)_(1812)) · data service · undated · scope: Germany (text, 1st ed., Berlin: Realschulbuchhandlung, pp. 238–250)
  - Quote 2: "gab ihm mit der Hand einen Stumpf in den Rücken. Da fuhr ihm der garstige Apfelgrütz, den es abgebissen hatte, aus dem Hals"
  - Flags: none [Also from the 1812 page: "der Prinz aber ließ ihn in sein Schloß tragen". Ashliman's English 1812 translation (https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/type0709.html) gives "hit her in the back with his hand". The script should use the 1857 stumble (F3) and mention the 1812 blow only as a side note, if at all.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (Ashliman grimm053 notes: "In the first edition the apple is dislodged when a servant, angry for having to carry Snow-White's coffin ... wherever the prince goes, strikes the sleeping princess" and "Beginning with the edition of 1819, the poisoned apple is dislodged when a servant accidentally stumbles"; Wikisource 1812 confirms the blow. Castle, anger and "lifted Snow-White upright" are confirmed on the type0709 page (cited in the flags): "The prince had it carried to his castle", "the servants who always had to carry the coffin to and fro became angry", "lifted Snow-White upright".)

- **Q5-F10** EDITION DIFFERENCE: in the 1812 first edition the woman invited to the wedding and made to dance to death in glowing iron shoes is Snow White's own "godless mother", not her stepmother.
  - Source: [Sneewittchen (Schneeweißchen) (1812), Wikisource (German)](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Sneewittchen_(Schneewei%C3%9Fchen)_(1812)) · data service · undated · scope: Germany (text, 1st ed.)
  - Quote: "Sneewittchens gottlose Mutter, auch eingeladen" / "da waren eiserne Pantoffeln im Feuer glühend gemacht, die mußte sie anziehen und darin tanzen"
  - Source 2: [Little Snow-White, version of 1812 (Ashliman, Folktexts type 709)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/type0709.html) · academic · site revised 2022-01 · scope: Germany (text, 1st ed.)
  - Quote 2: "Snow-White's godless mother was invited as well." / "they put a pair of iron shoes into the fire until they glowed"
  - Flags: none [Matches Q2's finding: mother in 1812, stepmother from 1819 on.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (both sources; dancing to death confirmed: "sich zu todt getanzt hatte" / "she could not stop until she had danced herself to death". Paraphrase: "glowing" for "glühend".)
