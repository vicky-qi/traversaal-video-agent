## Q3. Which cell chemistries are used in passenger electric cars sold in the latest full year, what share of new electric cars uses each, and how do they compare on energy stored per kilogram, cost per kWh and fire safety?

- **Q3-F1** In 2025, LFP batteries made up more than 55% of EV batteries deployed worldwide, up from nearly 50% in 2024.
  - Source: [IEA, Global EV Outlook 2026: Electric vehicle batteries](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2026/electric-vehicle-batteries) · international · published 2026 (month not stated on page) · scope: global
  - Quote: "In 2025, LFP batteries accounted for over 55% of EV batteries deployed globally, up from nearly 50% in 2024."
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [Measured as a share of battery capacity, not of cars sold. Per the page's chart note it covers all EV types except two- and three-wheelers, so vans, buses and trucks are included. [Benchmark Mineral Intelligence](https://source.benchmarkminerals.com/article/global-lithium-ion-battery-demand-rose-29-in-2025) separately reports that LFP was the fastest-growing chemistry in 2025 ("demand increasing by 48% year-on-year", all uses). That supports the direction but gives no share.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (IEA; the quoted sentence is on the page word for word. The chart note confirms that chemistry shares are based on the battery capacity of newly registered EVs and leave out two- and three-wheelers. Paraphrased: "made up" for "accounted for", "worldwide" for "globally".)

- **Q3-F2** In China, LFP made up 81.2% of battery installations in 2025 (625.3 GWh of 769.7 GWh), and nickel-based "ternary" (NCM/NCA) batteries made up 18.7%.
  - Source: [CnEVPost, "China EV battery installations in 2025" (data from the China Automotive Battery Innovation Alliance, CABIA)](https://cnevpost.com/2026/01/16/china-ev-battery-installations-in-2025/) · news (reporting official industry-alliance data) · published 2026-01 · scope: China
  - Quote: "LFP battery installations in 2025 totaled 625.3 GWh, accounting for 81.2% of total installations."
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [The page also says: "Ternary battery installations in 2025 reached 144.1 GWh, representing 18.7% of total installations." The figures are "power battery installations" across China's EV market. The page does not say whether buses and trucks are included.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (CnEVPost, 2026-01-16, CABIA data. All the numbers are on the page: the 769.7 GWh full-year total, 625.3 GWh and 81.2% for LFP, and 144.1 GWh and 18.7% for ternary. Paraphrased: "battery installations" for "power battery installations". The article text says only "ternary"; "nickel-based" is the research's gloss, and "NCM/NCA" appears only in the page's dataset metadata. WARN: the page does not say which vehicle types are counted, so do not present these as passenger-car shares. The two chemistry totals add up to 769.4 GWh, 0.3 GWh short of the stated total; the page does not explain the gap.)

- **Q3-F3** Outside China, almost 80% of EV batteries deployed in 2025 used nickel-based chemistries such as NMC, and most of the rest were LFP.
  - Source: [IEA, Global EV Outlook 2026: Electric vehicle batteries](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2026/electric-vehicle-batteries) · international · published 2026 (month not stated on page) · scope: global excluding China
  - Quote: "almost 80% of batteries used nickel‑containing chemistries" (cut off)
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE
  - Check: VERIFIED (IEA. The full sentence covers only chemistries deployed outside China in 2025, gives almost 80% as nickel-containing such as NMC, and says the remainder used LFP. The page says all of the rest was LFP, so "most of the rest" is a weaker claim and still safe. Paraphrased: "nickel-based" for "nickel-containing".)

- **Q3-F5** The newest LFP cells store up to about 205 watt-hours per kilogram, compared with about 265 Wh/kg for NMC cells (IEA figures as of 2026).
  - Source: [IEA, Global EV Outlook 2026: Electric vehicle batteries](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2026/electric-vehicle-batteries) · international · published 2026 (month not stated on page) · scope: global (technology figures)
  - Quote: "compared with up to 205 Wh/kg for the latest generation of LFP batteries and 265 Wh/kg" (cut off; continues "for lithium nickel cobalt manganese oxide (NMC) batteries")
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [These are cell-level figures: the sentence compares them with "the latest sodium-ion cells", which reach up to 175 Wh/kg. See Gaps for an older lab range that measures different cells.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (IEA. One sentence gives up to 175 Wh/kg for the latest sodium-ion cells, up to 205 Wh/kg for the latest generation of LFP and 265 Wh/kg for NMC. Paraphrased: "newest" for "latest generation", and "cells" for the page's "batteries" (the page uses "cells" only for the sodium-ion figure and does not say whether the LFP and NMC figures are cell-level or pack-level). WARN: the page's "up to" and "latest generation" apply only to the LFP figure, not to the NMC figure; do not present either number as a whole-pack figure.)

- **Q3-F6** In 2025, LFP battery packs averaged about $81 per kWh, against about $128 per kWh for NMC packs, across all uses.
  - Source: [BloombergNEF, "Lithium-Ion Battery Pack Prices Fall to $108 per Kilowatt-Hour, Despite Rising Metal Prices"](https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-transport/lithium-ion-battery-pack-prices-fall-to-108-per-kilowatt-hour-despite-rising-metal-prices-bloombergnef/) · industry · published 2025-12 · scope: global
  - Quote: "Average LFP battery pack prices across all segments came in at $81/kWh while nickel manganese cobalt (NMC) packs were at $128/kWh"
  - Source 2: [IEA, Global EV Outlook 2026: Electric vehicle batteries](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2026/electric-vehicle-batteries) · international · published 2026 (month not stated on page) · scope: global
  - Quote 2: "In 2025, LFP battery packs were more than 40% cheaper on average" (cut off; continues "than ... NMC alternatives per kWh")
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE for the dollar figures [The IEA confirms the gap only as a percentage. The BNEF dollar figures imply a gap of about 37% (see Calculation); the difference is under 10% and is not treated as a conflict. Both averages include stationary storage, and the IEA says this pulls LFP's average down because storage needs less energy density. Whether the IEA's figure draws on BNEF data is UNVERIFIED.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (BNEF press release, 2025-12-09. The page gives $81/kWh for LFP and $128/kWh for NMC, averaged across all segments. The sentence itself names no year; the release reports BNEF's 2025 battery price survey. Paraphrased: "averaged" for "Average … came in at", "all uses" for "all segments". The IEA page (Source 2) confirms the "more than 40% cheaper" wording and the stationary-storage effect, but not the dollar figures. WARN: these averages include stationary storage, so they are not EV-only prices. The IEA's "more than 40%" gap is larger than the roughly 37% implied by the BNEF figures, so do not put the two side by side as one number.)

- **Q3-F8** A note on the market leaders: NMC721, NMC811 and NCA together made up roughly 80% of the 2025 EV batteries that used cobalt-containing chemistries.
  - Source: [IEA, Global EV Outlook 2026: Electric vehicle batteries](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2026/electric-vehicle-batteries) · international · published 2026 (month not stated on page) · scope: global
  - Quote: "accounted for roughly 80% of 2025 EV battery deployment using cobalt‑containing chemistries" (cut off; the subject is NMC721, NMC811 and NCA)
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [This is technical detail. The script will probably need only the plain names "NMC" and "NCA", meaning nickel-rich recipes.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (IEA, footnote 2. Its subject is NMC721 and NMC811 together with NCA, and the quoted wording matches. WARN: "market leaders" is the research's framing, not the page's. The 80% is a share of cobalt-containing EV batteries only, not of all EV batteries.)

- **Q3-F9** Lab tests show a trade-off between energy and heat tolerance: cells that store more energy are less thermally stable. LFP cells tend to reach thermal runaway later than NMC cells, and less violently.
  - Source: [PowerUp, "NMC vs LFP: safety and performance in operation" (test data credited to the French research lab CEA-Liten)](https://powerup-technology.com/nmc-vs-lfp-safety-and-performance-in-operation/) · company · undated (page shows "4 years ago") · scope: global (lab tests)
  - Quote: "the higher the energy density, the lower the thermal stability of the cells" · LFP cells "tend to reach thermal runaway level later than" NMC cells (cut off)
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [This is qualitative only, with no temperatures in the text. The publisher is a battery-analytics firm spun off from CEA-Liten, and the page partly promotes its own product. The page also says neither chemistry is immune to thermal runaway. Better peer-reviewed sources were blocked; see Gaps.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (PowerUp. The page says that, globally, higher energy density means lower thermal stability. It also says LFP cells tend to reach thermal runaway later than NMC cells and tend to run away less violently. The tests were cell-level runs in adiabatic calorimeters with CEA-Liten. The page's own sentence has the typo "C/LPF" for LFP. Paraphrased: "trade-off" (not on the page), "store more energy" for "higher energy density", "heat tolerance" for "thermal stability". WARN: the page hedges with "globally" and "tend to", says both chemistries are subject to thermal runaway, and mixes cell formats and types in its sample. "Store more energy" means energy density, not a bigger battery.)
