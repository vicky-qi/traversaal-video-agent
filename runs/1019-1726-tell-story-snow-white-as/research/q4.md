## Q4. How many times does the queen try to kill Snow White, what disguise and object does she use each time, and how is Snow White saved after each attempt?

**Short answer:** After the huntsman fails her (see Q3), the queen comes to the dwarfs' cottage in disguise three times: first as an old peddler woman selling bodice laces, which she pulls so tight Snow White stops breathing; then as a different old woman with a poisoned comb; and finally as a peasant (country) woman with an apple poisoned only on its red half. The dwarfs save her the first two times, by cutting the laces and by pulling out the comb, but after the apple nothing they do helps, and she is revived only later when the piece of apple is jolted out of her throat while the coffin is carried. The film follows the final 1857 edition (Ashliman's translation, checked against Margaret Hunt's); the three attempts are the same in the 1812 first edition, but the antagonist there is Snow White's own mother, and the apple comes loose in a different way (a servant strikes her instead of stumbling).

**Worth showing:** The three visits as a repeating rhythm: a cloaked silhouette at the cottage door, each time in a new disguise; laces pulled tight around a paper figure, then snipped apart; a comb sliding into her hair, then pulled out; an apple cut in two, white half and red half, with the queen eating the white; the dwarfs gathered around a still figure, failing the third time; the coffin bearers stumbling and the apple piece dropping free.

## Gaps

- The count of "three" attempts is read from the text (three disguised visits); neither translation page states the number outright. If the huntsman order (Q3) is counted, the queen tries four times; the script should say "three times in disguise" to avoid ambiguity.
- The German original (Sneewittchen, 1857) was not opened in this run; both English translations agree on all plot points above.
- Ashliman's 1857 and 1812 translations are by the same translator, so the edition comparison relies on one translator's notes; the 1812 changes are stated on his type 709 page.
