## Q2. How are individual cells grouped and wired into modules and a full pack in a typical passenger electric car, and what do the battery management system and the cooling system monitor and control?

- **Q2-F1** Electric car battery packs are usually made of several modules, and each module contains a number of battery cells (as of 2024).
  - Source: [IEA, Global EV Outlook 2024: Trends in electric vehicle batteries](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2024/trends-in-electric-vehicle-batteries) · international · published 2024 · scope: global
  - Quote: "Battery packs used in EVs are typically made of a series of modules, each containing several battery cells."
  - Flags: none
  - Check: VERIFIED (IEA GEVO 2024; the quoted sentence is on the page word for word, in one of the page's notes. Paraphrased: "usually" for "typically", "several modules" for "a series of modules", "a number of" for "several".)

- **Q2-F2** Wiring cells end to end (in series) adds their voltages together, while wiring them side by side (in parallel) adds their capacity.
  - Source: [Battery University, BU-302: Series and Parallel Battery Configurations](https://batteryuniversity.com/article/bu-302-series-and-parallel-battery-configurations) · industry · updated 2021-10 · scope: global
  - Quote: "each cell adds its voltage potential to derive at the total terminal voltage."
  - Flags: none [same page on parallel: "Parallel connection attains higher capacity by adding up the total ampere-hour (Ah)." Battery University is run by Cadex Electronics.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (BU-302 says both: the series sentence in the quote and "Parallel connection attains higher capacity by adding up the total ampere-hour (Ah)." Paraphrased: "end to end" for series, "side by side" for parallel, "capacity" for ampere-hour capacity.)

- **Q2-F3** In lithium-ion packs, cells are first joined side by side into small groups, and those finished groups are then chained end to end.
  - Source: [Battery University, BU-302: Series and Parallel Battery Configurations](https://batteryuniversity.com/article/bu-302-series-and-parallel-battery-configurations) · industry · updated 2021-10 · scope: global
  - Quote: "the parallel strings are always made first; the completed parallel units are then placed in series."
  - Source 2: [GM TechLink, 2022 Bolt EUV and Bolt EV High-Voltage Battery Assembly](https://gm-techlink.com/?p=15088) · company · published 2021-07 · scope: US
  - Quote 2: "Three cells are welded together in parallel to form 96 cell groups."
  - Flags: none
  - Check: VERIFIED (BU-302. The full sentence begins "With Li-ion, the parallel strings are always made first", so the page ties this order to lithium-ion as the claim does; the quote leaves out those opening words. GM TechLink (Source 2) shows the same order in the Bolt: cells welded in parallel into groups, then groups "clamped together in series". Paraphrased: "side by side" for parallel, "small groups" for "parallel strings"/"parallel units", "chained end to end" for "placed in series".)

- **Q2-F4** The 2022 Chevrolet Bolt EV pack holds 288 lithium-ion cells of about 3.65 volts each, welded three at a time into 96 groups, which are connected in series to form 10 modules.
  - Source: [GM TechLink, 2022 Bolt EUV and Bolt EV High-Voltage Battery Assembly](https://gm-techlink.com/?p=15088) · company · published 2021-07 · scope: US
  - Quote: "The 288 individual lithium-ion battery cells of the drive motor battery are rated at approximately 3.65 volts each."
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [same page: "These cell groups are clamped together in series to form 10 cell modules." and "Two modules are physically assembled together to form a section, or row." The pack is 65 kWh per GM. Only GM's own document was opened; no independent teardown was reached.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (GM TechLink, dated 2021-07-29, covering the 2022 Bolt EV and Bolt EUV. The page says 288 cells "rated at approximately 3.65 volts each", "Three cells are welded together in parallel to form 96 cell groups" and "These cell groups are clamped together in series to form 10 cell modules." Paraphrased: "welded three at a time" for three cells welded together in parallel, "connected" for "clamped together". WARN: "welded three at a time" hides that the three cells in each group are wired in parallel, so say "three cells in parallel"; and the modules are not all the same size: the page says "Battery modules 5 and 7 have eight cell groups while the others contain 10", so do not say every module holds the same number of cells.)

- **Q2-F5** Chaining the cell groups in series gives the Bolt pack a nominal voltage of 350 volts, far above the roughly 3.65 volts of a single cell.
  - Source: [GM TechLink, 2022 Bolt EUV and Bolt EV High-Voltage Battery Assembly](https://gm-techlink.com/?p=15088) · company · published 2021-07 · scope: US
  - Quote: "The system's nominal voltage is 350 volts."
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [Battery University BU-302 (updated 2021-10) says in general that electric vehicles "are typically 450–500V" and need "more than 100 Li-ion cells connected in series". That is a general statement about other vehicles, not a measurement of the Bolt, so it is not a conflict; it does show pack voltage varies by car. See Gaps for the arithmetic check.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (GM TechLink gives "The system's nominal voltage is 350 volts" and cells of "approximately 3.65 volts each". The page does not state the cause in one sentence, but it describes one series circuit through all 96 groups: groups "clamped together in series" into modules, bus bars that "connect each battery section together in series", and a service disconnect that "physically interrupts the series circuit". 96 × 3.65 V = 350.4 V, which matches 350 V. Paraphrased: "chaining" for connecting in series.)

- **Q2-F6** Some newer packs skip modules and put cells straight into the pack ("cell-to-pack"), which cuts filler material and lets the pack store more energy for its size (as of 2024).
  - Source: [IEA, Global EV Outlook 2024: Trends in electric vehicle batteries](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2024/trends-in-electric-vehicle-batteries) · international · published 2024 · scope: global
  - Quote: "battery cells are assembled to build a pack without using modules, which reduces the need for inert materials and increases energy density." (returned in two segments)
  - Flags: none [same page: "In cell-to-chassis concepts, battery cells are used as part of the EV structure"]
  - Check: VERIFIED (IEA GEVO 2024. The full sentence, in a page note, reads "In the cell-to-pack configuration, battery cells are assembled to build a pack without using modules, which reduces the need for inert materials and increases energy density." The main text calls these "recent battery pack configurations" and says cell-to-pack is "already being adopted for LFP". Paraphrased: "newer" for "recent", "some" for "being adopted", "filler material" for "inert materials", "store more energy for its size" for "energy density".)

- **Q2-F7** The battery management system constantly measures voltage, current and temperature and uses them, with battery models, to estimate how full the battery is (state of charge) and how healthy it is (state of health).
  - Source: [Battery University, BU-908: Battery Management System (BMS)](https://batteryuniversity.com/article/bu-908-battery-management-system-bms) · industry · updated 2021-11 · scope: global
  - Quote: "The sensor reads voltage, current and temperature to estimate state-of-charge and detect anomalies."
  - Source 2: [Ionworks, Batteries 101: Battery management systems](https://docs.ionworks.com/guide/batteries-101/battery-management-systems) · company · undated · scope: global
  - Quote 2: "Calculates key metrics (State of Charge, State of Health, State of Power, State of Energy) using sensor data and models."
  - Flags: none [Real example: in the Bolt, GM says "The BECM collects temperature information from six sensors mounted to specific battery modules" plus "voltage from each cell group, through volt sense wires and total system current" (GM TechLink, 2021-07; the six-sensor count is SINGLE SOURCE). US DOE's AFDC says the onboard charger "monitors battery characteristics such as voltage, current, temperature, and state of charge while charging the pack" ([AFDC](https://afdc.energy.gov/vehicles/how-do-all-electric-cars-work), undated).]
  - Check: VERIFIED (Source 2, Ionworks. The page has Quote 2 word for word and also says the BMS "performs real-time calculations", "monitors temperature sensors distributed throughout the pack", and, after listing overvoltage, undervoltage, overcurrent and over-temperature, "The BMS must monitor all conditions simultaneously." Paraphrased: "constantly" for "real-time", "measures voltage, current and temperature" for monitoring those conditions (the page never uses "measure" for voltage and current), "how full" and "how healthy" for SoC and SoH. Source 1 (BU-908) does not verify the claim: its quoted sentence is about a separate "battery sensor" that "luxury cars are fitted with" (an electronic battery monitor on the battery clamp), not an EV battery management system. It estimates only state of charge, and the same caption says capacity assessment "is not possible".)

- **Q2-F8** Because no two cells are identical, cells in a series chain drift apart in charge, and the weakest cell limits the whole pack, so the battery management system balances them.
  - Source: [Ionworks, Batteries 101: Battery management systems](https://docs.ionworks.com/guide/batteries-101/battery-management-systems) · company · undated · scope: global
  - Quote: "In a series-connected pack, cells inevitably drift apart in their SoC because no two cells are identical."
  - Source 2: [Battery University, BU-302: Series and Parallel Battery Configurations](https://batteryuniversity.com/article/bu-302-series-and-parallel-battery-configurations) · industry · updated 2021-10 · scope: global
  - Quote 2: "a battery is only as strong as the weakest link in the chain."
  - Flags: none [Ionworks describes two ways to balance: "passive" balancing burns off a fuller cell's extra energy as heat through a resistor (simpler, more common); "active" balancing moves energy from fuller cells to emptier ones (more efficient, more complex).]
  - Check: VERIFIED (Ionworks. The page has the quote word for word, then "This means the pack's usable capacity is limited by its weakest cell, even if other cells have capacity remaining." Its cell-balancing section has the BMS doing the balancing, for example "the BMS dissipates its excess energy through a resistor as heat". BU-302 (Source 2) agrees on the weakest link: "This is especially critical in a series configuration because a battery is only as strong as the weakest link in the chain." Paraphrased: "drift apart in charge" for "drift apart in their SoC", "limits the whole pack" for limiting "the pack's usable capacity". WARN: the page says the weakest cell limits the pack's usable capacity; do not stretch this to everything about the pack.)

- **Q2-F9** The battery management system keeps the battery within safe limits for voltage, current and temperature, and disconnects it, using heavy-duty switches called contactors, if a limit is exceeded or a fault occurs.
  - Source: [Battery University, BU-908: Battery Management System (BMS)](https://batteryuniversity.com/article/bu-908-battery-management-system-bms) · industry · updated 2021-11 · scope: global
  - Quote: "it disconnects the battery if set limits are exceeded or if a failure occurs."
  - Source 2: [Ionworks, Batteries 101: Battery management systems](https://docs.ionworks.com/guide/batteries-101/battery-management-systems) · company · undated · scope: global
  - Quote 2: "Heavy-duty switches that can isolate the battery in emergencies"
  - Flags: none [Ionworks also: the BMS "Ensures the battery never operates outside safe limits for voltage, current, and temperature." and can "Limit or prevent charging at low temperatures" to avoid lithium plating.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (split support: each part is on one of the two cited pages, but neither page covers the whole claim. BU-908: "The BMS also provides protection when charging and discharging; it disconnects the battery if set limits are exceeded or if a failure occurs." It does not name contactors or say which limits. Ionworks: "Ensures the battery never operates outside safe limits for voltage, current, and temperature"; under the heading "Contactors": "Heavy-duty switches that can isolate the battery in emergencies", "Controlled by the BMS based on fault conditions"; fuses act "if the BMS or contactors fail to respond to an overcurrent event". The word "contactors" is in the heading, not in Quote 2. WARN: for over-temperature, Ionworks says the BMS cuts power ("De-rate power output if cells exceed safe limits") or limits charging rather than disconnecting, so do not imply every limit breach instantly cuts the battery off.)

- **Q2-F10** Temperature affects a battery's life, performance and safety (heat wears cells out faster, cold cuts available power and energy), so the cooling system keeps cells in a target temperature range and evens out temperature differences between cells; the battery electronics run the coolant pumps and fans based on cell temperatures.
  - Source: [NREL, Tools for Designing Thermal Management of Batteries in Electric Drive Vehicles (OSTI 1064502)](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1064502) · official · published 2013-02 · scope: global (general technology)
  - Quote: "Thermal management of battery packs in xEVs is essential to keep the cells in the desired temperature range" (cut off; continues "reduce cell-to-cell temperature variations, both of which impact life and performance.")
  - Source 2: [Ionworks, Batteries 101: Battery management systems](https://docs.ionworks.com/guide/batteries-101/battery-management-systems) · company · undated · scope: global
  - Quote 2: "Commands coolant pumps and fans based on cell temperatures"
  - Flags: none [Real example: the Bolt uses "An integrated, liquid-cooling tray circulating 1.82 gallons (6.9 liters) of DEXCOOL™ coolant" and "a separate heating element for warm-ups in colder climates" (GM TechLink, 2021-07; coolant volume is SINGLE SOURCE). NREL in [Tech Briefs, 2018-01](https://www.techbriefs.com/component/content/article/28125-energy-storage-thermal-management) adds that "battery overheating can even pose safety hazards, including fires." NREL fact sheet ([OSTI 1045077](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1045077), 2012-06): preheating or precooling the battery while still plugged in can "extend its driving range" and "improve battery life over the long term" (may help Q4).]
  - Check: VERIFIED (split support: NREL covers everything before the semicolon, Ionworks covers the part after it. The NREL abstract (Pesaran et al., February 2013) says: "Temperature has a significant impact on life, performance, and safety of lithium-ion battery technology", "High temperatures degrade Li-ion cells faster", "low temperatures reduce power and energy capabilities", and thermal management keeps cells "in the desired temperature range and also reduce cell-to-cell temperature variations". Ionworks: "The BMS controls the thermal management system:" and "Cooling: Commands coolant pumps and fans based on cell temperatures". Paraphrased: "wears cells out faster" for "degrade … faster", "cuts" for "reduce", "target" for "desired", "evens out temperature differences" for "reduce cell-to-cell temperature variations", "battery electronics" for "BMS". WARN: both sources say "thermal management", which also heats the pack in the cold (Ionworks mentions heaters; the Bolt has a separate heating element, per the Flags). Calling it only the "cooling system" suggests it only cools, so say "cooling and heating system" or "thermal management system".)
