## Q7. Which next-generation battery types have major automakers or battery makers announced for mass production, with what target dates, and what gains in range, charging time or cost do they claim?

**Short answer:** Four next-generation types have dated mass-production announcements from major companies. (1) All-solid-state: Toyota targets its first car in 2027–28, and BYD targets a first car from 2027 with mass production from 2030. (2) Sodium-ion: CATL's Naxtra went into its first car (Changan) in February 2026, with large-scale production now targeted for late 2026. (3) Ultra-fast-charging LFP: BYD's 1,000 kW platform (2025) and second-generation Blade battery (2026) are already in production. (4) Lithium manganese-rich: GM and LG target US production in 2028. The claimed gains are longer range and roughly 10-minute charging for solid-state (still unproven, and mass adoption is unlikely before 2030), better cold-weather performance for sodium-ion but slightly less energy than LFP, about 400 km added in 5 minutes for BYD, and about a third more energy than the best LFP cells for GM's LMR. The brief left "major" open, so I chose the world's biggest automakers and battery makers that have given dates (Japan, China and the US). All performance figures are company claims, not independent tests. Few of the companies gave a cost-per-kWh figure.

- **Q7-F1** Toyota plans to launch its first electric car with an all-solid-state battery in 2027–28 (stated as "by 2028" in the IEA's 2026 report), a target it repeated in October 2025.
  - Source: [ESS News: Sumitomo Metal Mining and Toyota collaborate to mass-produce cathode materials for solid-state batteries](https://www.ess-news.com/?p=6877) · news · published 2025-10 · scope: Japan / global
  - Quote: "Toyota aims to commercialize them in 2027-2028."
  - Source 2: [IEA, Global EV Outlook 2026: Electric vehicle batteries](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2026/electric-vehicle-batteries) · international · undated page (report cited as IEA 2026) · scope: global
  - Quote 2: "has announced plans to launch its first all-solid-state battery-powered vehicle by 2028"
  - Flags: none [Both sources report Toyota's own target. The IEA adds that solid-state advantages "have not yet been demonstrated in real-world applications".]
- **Q7-F2** Toyota's early solid-state cars are claimed to reach up to 1,000 km (621 miles) of range on a full charge and to charge in as little as about 10 minutes (claims reported in September 2024).
  - Source: [CBT News: Toyota to launch solid-state battery production by 2026](https://cbtnews.com/toyota-to-launch-solid-state-battery-production-by-2026/) · news · published 2024-09 · scope: Japan / global
  - Quote: "Early models could deliver up to 621 miles (1,000 km) of range on a full charge." … "Charging could be as fast as 10 minutes"
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [These are company claims repeated by the news outlet. No charge window (such as 10–80%) is given. Toyota's own technical release was not opened. ESS News (Q7-F1) confirms only in general terms that the batteries are expected to bring "increased driving range, shorter charging times, and higher output".]
- **Q7-F3** BYD plans to sell its first car with an all-solid-state battery from 2027 and to begin mass production from 2030, and Samsung has set similar timelines (as of the IEA's 2026 report).
  - Source: [IEA, Global EV Outlook 2026: Electric vehicle batteries](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2026/electric-vehicle-batteries) · international · undated page (report cited as IEA 2026) · scope: China / global
  - Quote: "plans to sell its first EV using all-SSB from 2027 and to begin mass production from 2030"
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [The IEA's 2025 edition said Toyota and BYD were "planning first mass production by 2027-2028". That is the same organization, so it is not a second source, and the 2026 edition pushes BYD's mass production to 2030.]
- **Q7-F4** CATL's chairman said in June 2026 that solid-state batteries are unlikely to reach millions of cars before 2030, and the IEA expects them to stay limited to premium cars until the early 2030s.
  - Source: [CnEVPost: CATL chairman flags long road to solid-state battery mass production](https://cnevpost.com/2026/06/24/catl-chairman-long-road-solid-state-battery-mass-production/) · news · published 2026-06 · scope: global
  - Quote: "mass adoption of solid-state batteries in millions of vehicles is unlikely before 2030."
  - Source 2: [IEA, Global EV Outlook 2026: Electric vehicle batteries](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2026/electric-vehicle-batteries) · international · undated page (report cited as IEA 2026) · scope: global
  - Quote 2: "they are expected to remain limited to premium segments until the first half of the 2030s"
  - Flags: none [The two make related but different points: CATL is talking about volume, the IEA about which car segments. CATL's chairman also rated the technology at only "level 4" on a 1-to-9 scale toward mass production. The IEA says early solid-state costs "are likely to be high", and that semi-solid-state batteries "are already commercial".]
- **Q7-F5** CATL's Naxtra sodium-ion battery, which uses sodium instead of lithium, stores up to 175 Wh per kg (as of 2025). That is close to the latest LFP batteries at up to 205 Wh/kg, according to the IEA.
  - Source: [CnEVPost: CATL unveils sodium-ion, 12C Shenxing, Freevoy dual-power batteries](https://cnevpost.com/2025/04/21/catl-sodium-ion-12c-shenxing-freevoy-dual-power-batteries/) · news · published 2025-04 · scope: China
  - Quote: "has a high energy density of 175 Wh/kg, the highest in the world for sodium-ion batteries."
  - Source 2: [IEA, Global EV Outlook 2026: Electric vehicle batteries](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2026/electric-vehicle-batteries) · international · undated page (report cited as IEA 2026) · scope: global
  - Quote 2: "can reach up to 175 Wh/kg, compared with up to 205 Wh/kg for the latest generation of LFP batteries"
  - Flags: none [The 175 Wh/kg figure comes from CATL, and the IEA may be repeating it. The 205 Wh/kg figure for LFP is from the IEA only. The IEA says sodium-ion's competitiveness with LFP is limited "at current lithium prices", so it does not support a cost advantage today.]
- **Q7-F6** CATL now says large-scale production of its sodium-ion batteries will come by the fourth quarter of 2026. That is later than the December 2025 date it gave in April 2025.
  - Source: [CarNewsChina: CATL to mass produce sodium-ion batteries in 2026, targeting 600 km range in the future](https://carnewschina.com/2026/04/22/catl-to-mass-produce-sodium-ion-batteries-in-2026-targeting-600-km-range-in-the-future/) · news · published 2026-04 · scope: China
  - Quote: "paving the way for large-scale production by the fourth quarter of this year."
  - Source 2: [CnEVPost: CATL unveils sodium-ion, 12C Shenxing, Freevoy dual-power batteries](https://cnevpost.com/2025/04/21/catl-sodium-ion-12c-shenxing-freevoy-dual-power-batteries/) · news · published 2025-04 · scope: China
  - Quote 2: "the Naxtra passenger car battery will be in mass production by December 2025."
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [The two sources give the earlier and the later target, so each date has one source. [ChargedEVs](https://chargedevs.com/newswire/catl-plans-to-mass-produce-naxtra-sodium-ion-ev-battery-packs-by-the-end-of-2025/) (2025-04) also reported "by the end of 2025". CATL says sodium-ion cars could reach 500–600 km of range "as the supply chain matures". That is a future goal, not a current figure.]
- **Q7-F7** The first car with the Naxtra battery is Changan's Nevo (Qiyuan) A06, unveiled in February 2026 with a 45 kWh pack rated for about 400 km and planned to go on sale in mid-2026. CATL and Changan claim it keeps over 90% of its capacity at -40°C.
  - Source: [CnEVPost: CATL's sodium-ion batteries begin deployment in production-ready passenger vehicles](https://cnevpost.com/2026/02/05/catl-sodium-batteries-begin-deployment-in-production-passenger-vehicles/) · news · published 2026-02 · scope: China
  - Quote: "EV models equipped with a 45-kWh pack achieve up to 400 kilometers of pure electric range."
  - Source 2: [CarNewsChina: Changan and CATL unveil world's first mass-produced sodium-ion passenger EV](https://carnewschina.com/2026/02/05/changan-and-catl-unveil-worlds-first-mass-produced-sodium-ion-passenger-ev/) · news · published 2026-02 · scope: China
  - Quote 2: "the battery maintains over 90% capacity retention at -40℃."
  - Flags: none [Both pages give 45 kWh and about 400 km ("up to" in CnEVPost, "exceeding" in CarNewsChina), and both give the over-90% figure at -40°C. Neither page clearly names the test cycle for the 400 km. The mid-2026 launch date is from CnEVPost only. Both pages say Changan claims nearly three times the discharge power of an LFP pack at -30°C.]
- **Q7-F8** In March 2025, BYD announced a 1,000 kW (1 megawatt) charging platform and a new Blade battery that it says can add about 400 km of range in five minutes. It debuted in the Han L and Tang L in China.
  - Source: [Paul Tan's Automotive News: BYD reveals 1000V Super e-Platform](https://paultan.org/2025/03/19/byd-reveals-1000v-super-e-platform/) · news · published 2025-03 · scope: China
  - Quote: "up to 1,000 kW (or 1MW) of charging power" … "400 km of range can be added in just five minutes"
  - Source 2: [Electrek: BYD Super e-Platform](https://electrek.co/?p=406162) · news · published 2025-03 · scope: China
  - Quote 2: "up to 400km (249 miles) of range in a mere five minutes"
  - Flags: none [Both outlets report BYD's own claim, which BYD measured on the Han L; it has not been independently tested. Paul Tan also says the Blade cells charge at a "10C" rate. [CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/02/24/byd-begins-large-scale-deployment-megawatt-flash-charging-facilities/) (2026-02) confirms the 1,000 kW figure and the March 2025 launch.]
- **Q7-F9** BYD's second-generation Blade battery, launched in March 2026 with 1,500 kW chargers, is claimed to charge from 10% to 70% in five minutes.
  - Source: [EV Infrastructure News: BYD begins Europe expansion of its 1,500kW Flash Chargers](https://www.evinfrastructurenews.com/ev-networks/byd-begins-europe-expansion-of-its-1-500kw-flash-chargers-as-it-hits-10-000-stations-in-china) · news · published 2026-08 · scope: China / Europe
  - Quote: "a 10% to 70% charge in five minutes" … "a 10% to 97% charge in nine minutes at room temperature."
  - Source 2: [CarNewsChina: BYD's flash offensive, 5-minute Blade charging](https://carnewschina.com/2026/03/08/byds-flash-offensive-5-minute-blade-charging-cracks-nios-swap-legacy/) · news · published 2026-03 · scope: China
  - Quote 2: "BYD's hardware achieves a 10% to 70% SOC in 5 minutes."
  - Flags: none for 10–70% in 5 minutes [The 10–97% in 9 minutes figure is SINGLE SOURCE. The cold-weather claims are worded differently: EV Infrastructure News gives 10–97% in about 12 minutes at -30°C, and CarNewsChina gives 20–97% in 12 minutes at -30°C. Different starting points mean this is not a conflict. CarNewsChina says the battery goes into the Song and Qin in Q2 2026 and the Dolphin and Seagull in H2 2026.]
- **Q7-F10** GM and LG Energy Solution plan to start US production of lithium manganese-rich (LMR) cells in 2028, which use more manganese and much less cobalt. GM claims about 33% more energy than the best LFP cells and targets over 400 miles of range in an electric truck (announced May 2025).
  - Source: [ChargedEVs: GM says its new LMR batteries will change the outlook for the EV market](https://chargedevs.com/newswire/gm-says-its-new-lmr-batteries-will-change-the-outlook-for-the-ev-market/) · news · published 2025-05 · scope: US
  - Quote: "aims to start commercial production of LMR (lithium-manganese-rich) prismatic cells in the US in 2028"
  - Source 2: [NBC News: GM unveils new 'groundbreaking' EV battery tech, aims to be first to market](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/autos/gm-unveils-new-groundbreaking-ev-battery-tech-aims-first-market-rcna206547) · news · published 2025-05 · scope: US
  - Quote 2: "33% higher energy density" … "the best-performing LFP cells" … "GM aims to offer more than 400 miles of range in an electric truck"
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE for the 33% and 400-mile figures [The 2028 date is in both sources (NBC: "by 2028"). ChargedEVs gives "up to 30 percent more energy density than today's typical cells" at "a similar cost". That is measured against a different baseline, so it is not a conflict. ChargedEVs says LMR cells are 60–70% manganese and under 2% cobalt. GM's own newsroom release was blocked (403).]

## Gaps

- Toyota's own announcement of its solid-state range and charging targets was not opened. The Toyota–Sumitomo joint release on Nasdaq timed out and the InsideEVs page returned 502, so Q7-F2 rests on one news page from 2024.
- Not covered with an opened source: Nissan, Mercedes-Benz/Factorial, Volkswagen PowerCo/QuantumScape, Samsung SDI specifics and SAIC semi-solid-state cars. The batterytechonline overview of 14 solid-state timelines returned 403. Search had run out (6 searches used).
- UNVERIFIED: Nissan targets mass production of all-solid-state batteries by the end of fiscal year 2028 (seen only in a search summary).
- UNVERIFIED: SAIC/MG has started deliveries of a semi-solid-state MG4 model (seen only in a search summary). The IEA does confirm in general terms that semi-solid-state batteries "are already commercial".
- UNVERIFIED: The Changan A06's 400 km is on China's CLTC test cycle, which equals about 328 km on WLTP (heise, not opened).
- UNVERIFIED: Sodium-ion batteries are expected to cost about 30% less than LFP. CarNewsChina attributes this to the IEA, but I did not open the IEA page it links. The IEA's 2026 report says sodium-ion's competitiveness is limited at current lithium prices. No sourced cost-per-kWh gain exists for any next-generation type in this file.
- Not confirmed: whether CATL's sodium-ion volume production and Changan A06 sales had actually started by October 2026.
- Not confirmed: whether GM's 2028 LMR date still holds. The IEA's 2026 report says GM, Ford and Stellantis "have recently scaled back their electrification ambitions", and I found no 2026 update.
- Left out: CATL's second-generation Shenxing LFP battery (April 2025: 800 km range, 12C peak charging, up to 1.3 MW). It is on the CnEVPost page cited in Q7-F5, but the page gives no mass-production date.
