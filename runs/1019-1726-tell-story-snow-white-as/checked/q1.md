## Q1. In the Grimms' published text of "Little Snow White" (Sneewittchen), what is the plot in order, scene by scene, and which edition is the standard source (first edition versus the final revised edition)?

- **Q1-F1** The Grimms' collection went through seven editions of the "large edition", from the first volume in December 1812 to the seventh in 1857, which is called the "Ausgabe letzter Hand" (the final edition they prepared themselves).
  - Source: [Staatsbibliothek zu Berlin, "Die Erstausgabe der Kinder- und Hausmärchen und die Auflagen bis 1857"](https://staatsbibliothek-berlin.de/die-staatsbibliothek/abteilungen/kinder-und-jugendbuecher/aktuelles/ausstellung/erstausgabe) · official (state library) · published undated · scope: Germany
  - Quote: "die siebente, die „Ausgabe letzter Hand“, wurde 1857 veröffentlicht." / "am 20. Dezember 1812, erschien der erste Band der Kinder- und Hausmärchen."
  - Source 2: [Wikisource (German), "Sneewittchen (1857)", source header](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Sneewittchen_(1857)) · data service (transcription of the 1857 print from scans) · published undated · scope: Germany
  - Quote 2: "Auflage: 7. Auflage (Ausgabe letzter Hand)" / "aus: Kinder- und Haus-Märchen Band 1 (1857), Große Ausgabe. S. 264-273"
  - Flags: none
  - Check: VERIFIED (Staatsbibliothek: both quotes found word for word; the page does not say "seven" outright, but "die siebente ... 1857" plus "vier weitere Auflagen der Großen Ausgabe" after 1837 make seven. Wikisource header confirms "7. Auflage (Ausgabe letzter Hand)". "The final edition they prepared themselves" is a fair gloss of "Ausgabe letzter Hand".)

- **Q1-F3** Opening (1857): in midwinter a queen sewing at an ebony-framed window pricks her finger, three drops of blood fall on the snow, and she wishes for a child white as snow, red as blood and black as the window frame; she has the daughter and dies as soon as the child is born.
  - Source: [D. L. Ashliman, "Little Snow-White" (1857)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published revised 2005-11 · scope: Germany (text)
  - Quote: "If only I had a child as white as snow, as red as blood, and as black as the wood in this frame." / "And as soon as the child was born, the queen died."
  - Source 2: [Wikisource (German), "Sneewittchen (1857)"](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Sneewittchen_(1857)) · data service · published undated · scope: Germany
  - Quote 2: "es fielen drei Tropfen Blut in den Schnee" / "Und wie das Kind geboren war, starb die Königin."
  - Flags: none
  - Check: VERIFIED (Ashliman: "in midwinter", "a frame of black ebony wood", "pricked her finger with her needle", "Three drops of blood fell into the snow", and both quotes found word for word. Wikisource quotes also present.)

- **Q1-F4** The mirror (1857): a year later the king takes a new, proud wife who owns a magic mirror; she asks it who is fairest in the land, and it answers that she is.
  - Source: [D. L. Ashliman, "Little Snow-White" (1857)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published revised 2005-11 · scope: Germany (text)
  - Quote: "A year later the king took himself another wife." / "Mirror, mirror, on the wall, / Who in this land is fairest of all?" / "You, my queen, are fairest of all."
  - Source 2: [Wikisource (German), "Sneewittchen (1857)"](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Sneewittchen_(1857)) · data service · published undated · scope: Germany
  - Quote 2: "Spieglein, Spieglein an der Wand, wer ist die schönste im ganzen Land?"
  - Flags: none
  - Check: VERIFIED (Ashliman: all quotes found, plus "proud and arrogant" and "She had a magic mirror." Wikisource quote present, with a line break after "Wand,".)

- **Q1-F5** The turn (1857): when Snow-White is seven years old she becomes more beautiful than the queen, and the mirror now names Snow-White as fairer, which fills the queen with envy.
  - Source: [D. L. Ashliman, "Little Snow-White" (1857)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published revised 2005-11 · scope: Germany (text)
  - Quote: "When she was seven years old she was as beautiful as the light of day, even more beautiful than the queen herself."
  - Source 2: [Wikisource (German), "Sneewittchen (1857)"](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Sneewittchen_(1857)) · data service · published undated · scope: Germany
  - Quote 2: "als es sieben Jahr alt war"
  - Flags: none [the age seven is a number the narration may say out loud; both sources are the same Grimm text, so the second source confirms the text, not an independent measurement]
  - Check: VERIFIED (Ashliman: quote found word for word; the mirror says "But Snow-White is a thousand times fairer than you" and the queen "turned yellow and green with envy". Wikisource "als es sieben Jahr alt war" present.)

- **Q1-F6** The huntsman (1857): the queen orders a huntsman to take Snow-White into the woods, kill her, and bring back her lungs and liver as proof; he lets her run, kills a young boar instead, and the queen has the boar's organs boiled with salt and eats them, thinking they are Snow-White's.
  - Source: [D. L. Ashliman, "Little Snow-White" (1857)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published revised 2005-11 · scope: Germany (text)
  - Quote: "Kill her, and as proof that she is dead bring her lungs and her liver back to me." / "The cook had to boil them with salt, and the wicked woman ate them,"
  - Source 2: [Wikisource (German), "Sneewittchen (1857)"](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Sneewittchen_(1857)) · data service · published undated · scope: Germany
  - Quote 2: "Du sollst es tödten, und mir Lunge und Leber zum Wahrzeichen mitbringen."
  - Flags: none [note: Margaret Hunt's 1884 English translation (Wikisource) renders this as the girl's "heart"; the German 1857 text says lungs and liver, so the script should say lungs and liver. This is a translation difference, not a conflict]
  - Check: VERIFIED (Ashliman: both quotes found; "Run away, you poor child." and "Just then a young boar came running by." confirm the escape and the swap. Wikisource quote present. WARN: the queen has the cook boil the organs and eats them; brief asks for this to be told plainly, not shown graphically.)

- **Q1-F7** The cottage (1857): Snow-White runs through the forest until evening, finds a small, very clean house, eats a little, and falls asleep in the seventh bed; it belongs to seven dwarfs who dig for ore in the mountains, and they let her stay if she keeps house, warning her to let no one in.
  - Source: [D. L. Ashliman, "Little Snow-White" (1857)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published revised 2005-11 · scope: Germany (text)
  - Quote: "so neat and clean that no one could say otherwise."
  - Source 2: [Wikisource (German), "Sneewittchen (1857)"](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Sneewittchen_(1857)) · data service · published undated · scope: Germany
  - Quote 2: "die sieben Zwerge, die in den Bergen nach Erz hackten und gruben"
  - Flags: none [the evening arrival, seventh bed and housekeeping deal were returned as summary from the Ashliman page; confirm wording there]
  - Check: VERIFIED (Ashliman, confirmed on the page: "just as evening was about to fall she saw a little house", "ate a few vegetables and a little bread from each little plate", "until finally the seventh one was just right", "the seven dwarfs who picked and dug for ore in the mountains", "If you will keep house for us...", "Do not let anyone in." Wikisource agrees: "das siebente", "laß ja niemand herein". Paraphrased words: "eats a little", "dig for ore" for "picked and dug for ore".)

- **Q1-F8** The three visits (1857): the queen comes back three times in disguise: first as an old peddler woman who laces Snow-White's bodice so tightly she falls as if dead; then as another old woman with a poisoned comb; then as a peasant woman with an apple poisoned only on its red half. The dwarfs revive her the first two times, but not after the apple.
  - Source: [D. L. Ashliman, "Little Snow-White" (1857)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published revised 2005-11 · scope: Germany (text)
  - Quote: "This time the dwarfs cannot awaken you." (the queen after the apple)
  - Source 2: [Wikisource (German), "Sneewittchen (1857)"](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Sneewittchen_(1857)) · data service · published undated · scope: Germany
  - Quote 2: "kleidete sich wie eine alte Krämerin" / "machte sie einen giftigen Kamm" / "verkleidete sich in eine Bauersfrau" / "daß der rothe Backen allein vergiftet war."
  - Flags: none [the number three is also confirmed by Gidwitz (ILA): "The queen comes to the dwarves' house not once, but three times"]
  - Check: VERIFIED (Ashliman: "old peddler woman", "pulled so quickly and so hard that Snow-White could not breathe", "as though she were dead", "taking the form of a different old woman", "she made a poisoned comb", "only the red half was poisoned", two revivals, and the quoted line. All four Wikisource quotes present. Note: the lead quote shows only the third visit; the rest is confirmed from the page.)

- **Q1-F9** The coffin and waking (1857): the dwarfs cannot bury her because she still looks alive, so they make a transparent glass coffin and set it on a mountain; a prince comes, the dwarfs give him the coffin out of pity, and when one of his servants stumbles on some brush while carrying it, the piece of poisoned apple shakes out of her throat and she wakes.
  - Source: [D. L. Ashliman, "Little Snow-White" (1857)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published revised 2005-11 · scope: Germany (text)
  - Quote: "But then it happened that one of them stumbled on some brush, and this dislodged from Snow-White's throat the piece of poisoned apple"
  - Source 2: [Wikisource (German), "Sneewittchen (1857)"](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Sneewittchen_(1857)) · data service · published undated · scope: Germany
  - Quote 2: "einen durchsichtigen Sarg von Glas machen" / "sie über einen Strauch stolperten, und von dem Schüttern fuhr der giftige Apfelgrütz" (cut off)
  - Flags: none
  - Check: VERIFIED (Ashliman: "she still looked as fresh as a living person", "transparent glass coffin", "put the coffin outside on a mountain", "the good dwarfs felt pity for him and gave him the coffin", and the stumble quote. WARN: Ashliman says "one of them" stumbled, the German says "sie" (they) stumbled; "the servants stumbled" fits both. Quote 2 is cut off.)

- **Q1-F10** The ending (1857): the stepmother is invited to the wedding, recognizes the young queen as Snow-White, and is made to step into iron shoes heated in burning coals and dance until she falls down dead. **Edition difference:** in the 1812 first edition the queen is Snow-White's own mother (no stepmother), and the apple is knocked loose when a servant angry at carrying the coffin strikes her in the back, not by a stumble.
  - Source: [D. L. Ashliman, "Little Snow-White" (1857)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published revised 2005-11 · scope: Germany (text)
  - Quote: "She was forced to step into the red-hot shoes and dance until she fell down dead." / "Snow-White's jealous antagonist is her own mother, not a stepmother." (end note on 1812)
  - Source 2: [Wikisource (German), "Sneewittchen (1857)"](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Sneewittchen_(1857)) · data service · published undated · scope: Germany
  - Quote 2: "Da mußte sie in die rothglühenden Schuhe treten und so lange tanzen, bis sie todt zur Erde fiel."
  - Source 3 (1812 differences): [D. L. Ashliman, "Snow-White" type 709 tales, with the 1812 Grimm text](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/type0709.html) · academic · published undated (translation © 1998-2002) · scope: Germany (text); and [Gidwitz, ILA blog](https://www.literacyworldwide.org/blog/literacy-now/2013/10/24/the-twisted-history-of-snow-white) · news · 2013-10
  - Quote 3: "Beginning with the edition of 1819, the poisoned apple is dislodged when a servant accidentally stumbles while carrying the coffin" (Ashliman) / "in the first published edition of the story, in 1812, there is no step-mother." (Gidwitz)
  - Flags: none [EDITION FLAG for the script: follow 1857 consistently, i.e. stepmother, stumbling servant. The 1812 text also has the iron-shoe ending and the lungs-and-liver order, per the type 709 page]
