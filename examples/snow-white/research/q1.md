## Q1. In the Grimms' published text of "Little Snow White" (Sneewittchen), what is the plot in order, scene by scene, and which edition is the standard source (first edition versus the final revised edition)?

**Short answer:** The Grimms published seven editions of their tale collection between 1812 and 1857; the seventh (1857) is the "Ausgabe letzter Hand", the last edition the brothers prepared themselves, and it is the text most readers and translations use. **Choice made: the film follows the final 1857 edition**, in D. L. Ashliman's English translation (University of Pittsburgh), checked against the German 1857 text on German Wikisource. In order, the 1857 plot runs: a queen pricks her finger and wishes for a child, then dies at the birth; the king remarries a vain queen with a magic mirror; when Snow-White is seven the mirror names her fairer; the queen orders a huntsman to kill her and bring back her lungs and liver; he spares her and she reaches the seven dwarfs' cottage; the queen comes three times in disguise (laces, poisoned comb, poisoned apple); the dwarfs lay her in a glass coffin; a prince takes the coffin, a servant stumbles, the apple piece comes loose; at the wedding the stepmother is made to dance in red-hot iron shoes until she dies. Two points differ in the 1812 first edition (flagged in Q1-F10): the villain is Snow-White's own mother, and the apple is knocked loose when a servant strikes her in the back.

**Worth showing:** three drops of blood falling onto snow beside a black window frame; the mirror answering each morning, then changing its answer when the girl turns seven; a small figure running deeper into the forest until a cottage appears; the queen returning three times in three disguises with three objects (laces, comb, half-red apple); a transparent glass coffin on a mountain; a coffin-bearer stumbling and the apple piece jolting loose; iron shoes heating over coals.

## Gaps

- The claim that 1857 is "the standard source" rests on its status as the Grimms' own final edition (Staatsbibliothek, Wikisource header) and on Gidwitz comparing the 1857 text with the familiar film. I did not open a scholarly page that states outright that most English translations follow 1857. Britannica's "Grimm's Fairy Tales" page returned 403.
- UNVERIFIED: Margaret Hunt's 1884 translation was made from a late (1857-based) edition; her rendering of "heart" instead of "lungs and liver" suggests a softened or different source wording. Not confirmed on any page I opened.
- UNVERIFIED: the Wikipedia statement (from search results only) that Jack Zipes links the stepmother change to the Grimms holding "motherhood sacred". Not opened; relevant to Q2, not Q1.
- The Ashliman type 709 page (1812 text) returned summarized rather than verbatim text for the servant striking Snow-White; the fact-checker should confirm that line on the page.
- The English Wikisource page for Hunt's translation would not return verbatim quotes, so it is not cited as a source; it was used only to spot the "heart" translation difference.
