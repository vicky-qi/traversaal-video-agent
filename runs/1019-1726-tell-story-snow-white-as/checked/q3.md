## Q3. What exactly does the queen order the huntsman to do and to bring back, and how does Snow White escape?

- **Q3-F1** In the final 1857 Grimm text, the queen orders the huntsman to take Snow White into the woods because she never wants to see her again, and to kill her.
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White, tr. D. L. Ashliman (University of Pittsburgh)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published 2002-2005 (copyright dates) · scope: Germany (text of KHM 1857, no. 53)
  - Quote: "Take Snow-White out into the woods. I never want to see her again."
  - Source 2: [Sneewittchen (1857), German Wikisource](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Sneewittchen_(1857)) · official document (primary text transcription, proofread against scans) · published undated · scope: Germany (KHM 7th ed., Göttingen: Dieterich, 1857)
  - Quote 2: "bring das Kind hinaus in den Wald, ich wills nicht mehr vor meinen Augen sehen."
  - Flags: none
  - Check: VERIFIED (both sources; Ashliman: "Take Snow-White out into the woods. I never want to see her again." and "Kill her, and as proof..."; German matches. Paraphrase: "because" links two adjacent sentences the text places side by side, which is a fair reading.)

- **Q3-F2** As proof that Snow White is dead, the queen demands her lungs and her liver (1857 final edition).
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White, tr. D. L. Ashliman](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published 2002-2005 · scope: Germany (KHM 1857)
  - Quote: "Kill her, and as proof that she is dead bring her lungs and her liver back to me."
  - Source 2: [Sneewittchen (1857), German Wikisource](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Sneewittchen_(1857)) · official document (primary text) · undated · scope: Germany (KHM 1857)
  - Quote 2: "Du sollst es tödten, und mir Lunge und Leber zum Wahrzeichen mitbringen."
  - Flags: none [Translation note, not a conflict: Margaret Hunt's 1884 English translation (Project Gutenberg #5314) has "bring me back her heart as a token", and the boar's "heart" later. The German original says lungs and liver ("Lunge und Leber"), so follow that.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (both sources, exact wording on both pages. WARN: say "lungs and liver", not "heart"; viewers who know the Hunt translation or popular versions expect "heart".)

- **Q3-F3** The first edition (1812) gives the same order and the same proof, lungs and liver, and here the queen says outright that she will cook and eat them.
  - Source: [Snow-White and other tales of type 709: "Little Snow-White, version of 1812", tr. D. L. Ashliman](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/type0709.html) · academic · published 1998-2002 (copyright dates) · scope: Germany (KHM 1st ed., Berlin 1812, v. 1, no. 53, pp. 238-50)
  - Quote: "As proof that she is dead bring her lungs and her liver back to me. I shall cook them with salt and eat them."
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [only English translation of the 1812 text opened; the same page says the 1812 order is to "stab her to death" at "a remote spot". In 1812 the queen is Snow White's own mother; see Q2.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (type0709 page, quote exact; 1812 order reads "Take Snow-White out into the woods to a remote spot, and stab her to death." Citation matches: 1st ed., Berlin: Realschulbuchhandlung, 1812, vol. 1, no. 53, pp. 238-50. WARN: "same order" is broadly true but the 1812 wording adds "a remote spot" and "stab her to death"; and the 1812 speaker is Snow White's own mother, so do not call her the stepmother in a sentence about 1812.)

- **Q3-F4** The huntsman takes her into the woods and draws his hunting knife to stab her heart, but she starts to cry and begs him to let her live, promising to run into the wild woods and never come back.
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White, tr. D. L. Ashliman](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published 2002-2005 · scope: Germany (KHM 1857)
  - Quote: "Oh, dear huntsman, let me live. I will run into the wild woods and never come back."
  - Source 2: [Sneewittchen (1857), German Wikisource](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Sneewittchen_(1857)) · official document (primary text) · undated · scope: Germany (KHM 1857)
  - Quote 2: "ach, lieber Jäger, laß mir mein Leben; ich will in den wilden Wald laufen und nimmermehr wieder heim kommen."
  - Flags: none [knife detail: Ashliman "about to stab it into her innocent heart"; German "Sneewittchens unschuldiges Herz durchbohren wollte"]
  - Check: VERIFIED (both sources; Ashliman: "He took out his hunting knife and was about to stab it into her innocent heart when she began to cry"; German "den Hirschfänger gezogen hatte". Note: the German says "nimmermehr wieder heim kommen" (never come home again); Ashliman's "never come back" is fine.)

- **Q3-F5** The huntsman spares her because she is so beautiful and tells her to run. He thinks the wild animals will soon eat her anyway, but feels as if a stone has fallen from his heart because he does not have to kill her.
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White, tr. D. L. Ashliman](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published 2002-2005 · scope: Germany (KHM 1857)
  - Quote: "Because she was so beautiful the huntsman took pity on her, and he said, 'Run away, you poor child.'"
  - Source 2: [Sneewittchen (1857), German Wikisource](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Sneewittchen_(1857)) · official document (primary text) · undated · scope: Germany (KHM 1857)
  - Quote 2: "Und weil es so schön war, hatte der Jäger Mitleiden und sprach „so lauf hin, du armes Kind.“"
  - Flags: none [stone line: Ashliman "it was as if a stone had fallen from his heart, for he would not have to kill her"]
  - Check: VERIFIED (both sources together; Ashliman: "He thought, 'The wild animals will soon devour you anyway,' but still it was as if a stone had fallen from his heart." The "because he does not have to kill her" part is confirmed by the German: "als wär ein Stein von seinem Herzen gewälzt, weil er es nicht zu tödten brauchte." Paraphrase: "eat" for "devour".)

- **Q3-F6** To fool the queen, the huntsman kills a young boar that runs by, cuts out its lungs and liver, and takes them back as proof of Snow White's death.
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White, tr. D. L. Ashliman](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published 2002-2005 · scope: Germany (KHM 1857)
  - Quote: "Just then a young boar came running by. He killed it, cut out its lungs and liver,"
  - Source 2: [Sneewittchen (1857), German Wikisource](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Sneewittchen_(1857)) · official document (primary text) · undated · scope: Germany (KHM 1857)
  - Quote 2: "Und als gerade ein junger Frischling daher gesprungen kam, stach er ihn ab,"
  - Flags: none [The 1812 version has the same boar substitution: type0709.html, "He killed it, cut out its lungs and liver, and took them back to the queen as proof of Snow-White's death."]
  - Check: VERIFIED (both sources; Ashliman continues "and took them back to the queen as proof of Snow-White's death"; German "nahm Lunge und Leber heraus, und brachte sie als Wahrzeichen der Königin mit". Paraphrase: "to fool the queen" is implied, not stated.)

- **Q3-F7** The cook boils the organs with salt and the queen eats them, believing she has eaten Snow White's lungs and liver.
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White, tr. D. L. Ashliman](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published 2002-2005 · scope: Germany (KHM 1857)
  - Quote: "The cook had to boil them with salt, and the wicked woman ate them, supposing that she had eaten Snow-White's lungs and liver."
  - Source 2: [Sneewittchen (1857), German Wikisource](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Sneewittchen_(1857)) · official document (primary text) · undated · scope: Germany (KHM 1857)
  - Quote 2: "Der Koch mußte sie in Salz kochen, und das boshafte Weib aß sie auf"
  - Flags: none [The brief says violence is told plainly but not shown graphically; this is the darkest detail in the scene. In 1812 the queen cooks them herself: "She cooked them with salt and ate them" (type0709.html).]
  - Check: VERIFIED (both sources, exact wording. The 1812 note in the flags is also confirmed on type0709.html: "She cooked them with salt and ate them".)

- **Q3-F8** Alone and frightened in the great forest, Snow White runs over sharp stones and through thorns; wild animals leap at her but do her no harm. She runs as far as her feet will carry her, and just before evening she finds a little house and goes in to rest.
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White, tr. D. L. Ashliman](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published 2002-2005 · scope: Germany (KHM 1857)
  - Quote: "She ran over sharp stones and through thorns, and wild animals jumped at her, but they did her no harm."
  - Source 2: [Sneewittchen (1857), German Wikisource](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Sneewittchen_(1857)) · official document (primary text) · undated · scope: Germany (KHM 1857)
  - Quote 2: "Da fieng es an zu laufen und lief über die spitzen Steine und durch die Dornen"
  - Flags: none [small wording point: the German says the animals "sprangen an ihm vorbei" (sprang past her) where Ashliman has "jumped at her"; "leapt past her" is closest to the German. Arrival: Ashliman "just as evening was about to fall she saw a little house"; German "bis es bald Abend werden wollte, da sah es ein kleines Häuschen".]
  - Check: VERIFIED (Ashliman: "all alone in the great forest, and she was so afraid", "She ran as far as her feet could carry her", "just as evening was about to fall she saw a little house and went inside in order to rest"; German confirms stones, thorns and the house at evening. Paraphrase: "frightened" for "so afraid", "leap" for "jumped". WARN: the German says the animals "sprangen an ihm vorbei, aber sie thaten ihm nichts" (sprang past her and did nothing to her); "leapt past her" fits both texts, "attacked her" would not.)
