## Q6. Which of these Grimm details are changed or left out in the best-known modern film adaptation, according to reputable folklore scholarship or major publications?

- **Q6-F1** Disney's film dropped the queen's first two attempts on Snow White's life and kept only the poisoned apple, so the queen meets Snow White just once (film released 1937).
  - Source: [Pallant and Holliday (eds.), *Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs: New Perspectives on Production, Reception, Legacy*, Ch. 1 "From Caligari to Disney" (Internet Archive text of the open-access edition)](https://archive.org/stream/oapen-20.500.12657-58858/9781501351204_djvu.txt) · academic · published 2021 · scope: the 1937 film
  - Quote: "as Disney removes the queen's first two attempts to kill Snow White"
  - Source 2: Same book, Ch. 8 (a different chapter author; same URL as above) · academic · published 2021 · scope: the 1937 film
  - Quote 2: "with only one meeting taking place between the heroine and villainess"
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [both quotes come from one edited book, so they are not fully independent. The three Grimm attempts are confirmed separately in Q6-F2.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (Source 1: Ch. 1 "From Caligari to Disney" says Disney "removes the queen's first two attempts to kill Snow White", and Snow White then eats the apple, which she believes is a "magic wishing apple". Source 2: Ch. 8 "Framing Snow White" has the "only one meeting" line verbatim. "Kept only the poisoned apple" is a fair reading of Ch. 1. WARN: "first two attempts" means the two disguised visits (laces and comb). It does not cover the huntsman order, which the Grimm text also has. Say "the first two disguised visits", not "every attempt but one". "Meets just once" means a single face-to-face meeting.)

- **Q6-F2** In the Grimm text (final 1857 edition) the queen visits three times in disguise: first she laces Snow White too tightly, then she uses a poisoned comb, and last the poisoned apple.
  - Source: [D. L. Ashliman, "Little Snow-White" (Grimm KHM 53), University of Pittsburgh folktexts](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · updated 2005-11 · scope: Grimm text, 1857 edition
  - Quote: "Child, how you look! Come, let me lace you up properly." … "comb your hair properly" … "Look, I'll cut the apple in two"
  - Source 2: [Pallant and Holliday (eds.), Ch. 8](https://archive.org/stream/oapen-20.500.12657-58858/9781501351204_djvu.txt) · academic · published 2021 · scope: Grimm text
  - Quote 2: "the Queen visits Snow White on three occasions and lives long enough to attend Snow White's wedding to the prince"
  - Flags: none
  - Check: VERIFIED (both sources. Ashliman gives all three disguised visits in order: old peddler woman with bodice laces, a different old woman with a poisoned comb, peasant woman with the apple. The three quotes match the page. Ch. 8 has Quote 2 verbatim.)

- **Q6-F3** In the Disney film the prince wakes Snow White from the queen's "deathlike spell" with "love's first kiss" (film premiered 21 December 1937).
  - Source: [D23 (official Disney fan club), "Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (film)"](https://d23.com/a-to-z/snow-white-and-the-seven-dwarfs-film/) · company · undated · scope: the 1937 film
  - Quote: "deathlike spell" … "love's first kiss" … "Premiered on December 21, 1937, at the Carthay Circle Theater in Hollywood."
  - Source 2: [Laura O'Flanagan (Dublin City University), The Conversation, "Snow White: this opportunity to empower Disney's first princess falls short at every turn"](https://theconversation.com/snow-white-this-opportunity-to-empower-disneys-first-princess-falls-short-at-every-turn-253064) · news (academic author) · published 2025-03 · scope: the 1937 and 2025 films
  - Quote 2: "the kiss Snow White receives while asleep"
  - Flags: none
  - Check: VERIFIED (Source 1, D23: the Prince "awakens her from the wicked Queen's deathlike spell by 'love's first kiss'", and the page has the premiere line verbatim. Source 2 loaded with the stated author and date (2025-03-26) and has the quote verbatim. The book's Ch. 1 also says "the Prince awakens Snow White with a kiss".)

- **Q6-F4** In the Grimm text there is no kiss. The poisoned piece of apple comes loose when one of the prince's servants stumbles while carrying the coffin. In the 1812 first edition, a servant angry at carrying the coffin strikes her instead.
  - Source: [D. L. Ashliman, "Little Snow-White", University of Pittsburgh](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · updated 2005-11 · scope: Grimm text, 1857 edition, with notes on 1812
  - Quote: "stumbled on some brush"
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [for the Grimm side; the script should check this against Q5's sources. The 1812 version differs (a servant strikes her), so keep to one edition.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (Ashliman: the 1857 text has no kiss, and "one of them stumbled on some brush" refers to the servants carrying the coffin. The end note reads "In the first edition the apple is dislodged when a servant, angry for having to carry Snow-White's coffin ... strikes the sleeping princess." The quote is only a short fragment; the full line is above. WARN: the German 1857 text says "they" (the servants) stumbled (see Q5-F3), so "the servants stumbled" is the safer wording. Keep the 1812 blow as a clearly labelled side note so the two editions are not mixed.)

- **Q6-F6** The Grimm dwarfs have no names: the text calls them "the first one", "the second" and so on. Disney gave each dwarf a name and a distinct personality.
  - Source: [D. L. Ashliman, "Little Snow-White", University of Pittsburgh](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · updated 2005-11 · scope: Grimm text, 1857 edition
  - Quote: "The first one said"
  - Source 2: [Pamela C. O'Brien, "Dwarfland", Ch. 7 in Pallant and Holliday (eds.)](https://archive.org/stream/oapen-20.500.12657-58858/9781501351204_djvu.txt) · academic · published 2021 · scope: the 1937 film
  - Quote 2: "Disney gives each of the dwarfs a name and distinct personality."
  - Flags: none [the Disney dwarfs' names (Doc, Grumpy, Happy, Sleepy, Bashful, Sneezy, Dopey) are in Gaps as UNVERIFIED]
  - Check: VERIFIED (both. Ashliman: the dwarfs appear only as "the seven dwarfs" and by number ("The first one", "The seventh one"). They are never named. Ch. 7, "Dwarfland: Marketing Disney's folly" by Pamela C. O'Brien, has Quote 2 verbatim. WARN: the seven Disney names are not verified, so they must not be spoken or shown.)

- **Q6-F7** In the Grimm tale Snow White is barely more than a child, but Disney's studio decided she had to start the film as at least a teenager.
  - Source: [Pallant and Holliday (eds.), *Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs: New Perspectives*](https://archive.org/stream/oapen-20.500.12657-58858/9781501351204_djvu.txt) · academic · published 2021 · scope: Grimm text and the 1937 film
  - Quote: "In the Grimms' version of this very ancient tale, Snow White is very young — hardly more than a child" … "the character needed to begin the story as at least a teenager"
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [the exact Grimm age ("seven years old") was not confirmed on a page I opened; see Gaps]
  - Check: VERIFIED (Ch. 5, "Making it Disney's Snow White" by Amy M. Davis. Both quotes are on the page. The full sentence reads "Naturally, by the 1930s, making Snow White of a plausible age to marry meant that the character needed to begin the story as at least a teenager." Paraphrases: "barely" for "hardly", and "Disney's studio decided" for the book's "needed to", which it says in a chapter about Disney's choices. WARN: the book gives the reason as making her of a plausible age to marry. Do not give a Disney age (such as 14), which is not verified. Grimm's "seven years old" is a separate fact (Q1-F5), and it marks when the mirror first names her, not her age in later scenes.)

- **Q6-F8** Both the Disney film and the final Grimm edition make the villain Snow White's stepmother. In the Grimms' 1812 first edition she was Snow White's own mother, and the Grimms made her a stepmother from the 1819 second edition on.
  - Source: [D. L. Ashliman, "Little Snow-White", footnotes, University of Pittsburgh](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · updated 2005-11 · scope: Grimm text, 1812 vs 1819+ editions
  - Quote: "her own mother, not a stepmother"
  - Source 2: [D23, "Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (film)"](https://d23.com/a-to-z/snow-white-and-the-seven-dwarfs-film/) · company · undated · scope: the 1937 film
  - Quote 2: Disney side only: Snow White hides "to escape her stepmother" (paraphrase from the processed text; check exact wording)
  - Flags: none [Source 2 confirms only the Disney half. This is not a Disney change if the film follows the final Grimm edition.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (Ashliman's notes: "Snow-White's jealous antagonist is her own mother, not a stepmother" (first edition), and "Beginning with the edition of 1819, the Grimms add the statement that Snow-White's mother died during childbirth" and "that her father remarried". The 1857 text says "stepmother". D23 confirms the Disney half. Its actual wording is that Snow White hides "from her stepmother, the wicked Queen"; Quote 2 is a paraphrase of that.)
