## Q6. Which of these Grimm details are changed or left out in the best-known modern film adaptation, according to reputable folklore scholarship or major publications?

**Short answer:** The best-known modern adaptation is taken to be Walt Disney's animated *Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs* (premiered December 1937). The Grimm side is the final edition (1857) in D. L. Ashliman's translation, so that it lines up with Q1–Q5. Compared with that text, the film cuts the queen's first two attempts (the laces and the comb) and keeps only the poisoned apple. It wakes Snow White with "love's first kiss" instead of a servant stumbling while carrying the coffin. It replaces the red-hot iron shoes at the wedding with the queen falling from rocks in a storm. It gives the unnamed Grimm dwarfs names and personalities, and it makes Snow White a teenager rather than "hardly more than a child". Both versions make the villain a stepmother: the Grimms' 1812 first edition made her Snow White's own mother, but they changed that from 1819, so this is not a Disney change.

**Worth showing:** A split page with the Grimm half and the familiar half. On the Grimm half, the queen knocks three times (laces, comb, apple); on the familiar half she knocks once, with the apple. Coffin bearers stumble and the apple piece flies loose, set against a single kiss. Glowing red-hot iron shoes, set against a cloaked figure falling from rocks in a storm. Seven identical blank dwarf silhouettes that each take on a different shape and a name.

## Gaps

- No major general publication (BBC, NYT, Smithsonian, Britannica) could be opened: Britannica and Smithsonian returned 403. The comparison rests on one open-access academic book (Bloomsbury, 2021), the Ashliman translation, Disney's own D23 entry and one article in The Conversation.
- The Library of Congress National Film Registry essay by J. B. Kaufman (https://www.loc.gov/static/programs/national-film-preservation-board/documents/snow_white_dwarfs.rev.pdf) was fetched, but the PDF text could not be extracted, so it is not cited.
- UNVERIFIED: In the Disney film the huntsman is told to bring back Snow White's heart in a box and brings a pig's heart; in Grimm the queen asks for her lungs and liver, which she eats. Only Wikipedia confirmed the Disney "heart in a box" detail. The Grimm "lungs and her liver" wording appears on the Ashliman page; see the Q3 research.
- UNVERIFIED: Disney's dwarfs are named Doc, Grumpy, Happy, Sleepy, Bashful, Sneezy and Dopey (Wikipedia only).
- UNVERIFIED: In Disney the queen tries to roll a boulder onto the dwarfs before lightning strikes the cliff (Wikipedia only).
- UNVERIFIED: In Grimm Snow White is seven years old when the mirror first names her the fairest; Disney's is about 14 (blogs and The Vintage News only).
- UNVERIFIED: In Disney the prince meets Snow White early in the film, while in Grimm he appears only at the coffin (Wikipedia and blogs only; the Bloomsbury book says only that "It falls to the prince to rescue her").
- The 2025 live-action remake was not compared in detail: the brief asks for "the best-known" adaptation, and the 1937 film was chosen.
