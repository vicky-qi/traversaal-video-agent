## Q4. How many times does the queen try to kill Snow White, what disguise and object does she use each time, and how is Snow White saved after each attempt?

- **Q4-F1** In the final (1857) edition, the queen's first visit is disguised as an old peddler woman selling bodice laces.
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White (D. L. Ashliman translation, 1857 edition)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published updated 2005-11 · scope: Grimm text, 1857 edition
  - Quote: "Coloring her face, she disguised herself as an old peddler woman, so that no one would recognize her." / "Bodice laces in all colors."
  - Source 2: [Household Tales by Brothers Grimm, tr. Margaret Hunt (Project Gutenberg)](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/5314/5314-h/5314-h.htm) · academic · published undated · scope: Grimm text, final edition
  - Quote 2: "dressed herself like an old peddler-woman, and no one could have known her." / "stay-laces of all colours,"
  - Flags: none [the two are independent translations of the same German text; Hunt says "stay-laces", Ashliman "bodice laces"]
  - Check: VERIFIED (Ashliman; both quotes found verbatim, page says it translates the "final edition (Berlin, 1857), no. 53". Hunt quotes also found verbatim.)

- **Q4-F2** The queen laces Snow White so tightly that she cannot breathe and falls as if dead.
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White (Ashliman)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published updated 2005-11 · scope: 1857 edition
  - Quote: "Child, how you look! Come, let me lace you up properly." / "lying on the ground, not moving at all, as though she were dead."
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE [the plot point is confirmed by Hunt's text in Q4-F3, which describes her coming back to life after the laces are cut; quote wording is from WebFetch's summary, check live page]
  - Check: VERIFIED (Ashliman: "the old woman pulled so quickly and so hard that Snow-White could not breathe" and "lying on the ground, not moving at all, as though she were dead." The quoted lines do not themselves show the "cannot breathe" part; the sentence above does. Hunt's text also has "laced so quickly and so tightly that Snow-white lost her breath and fell down as if dead.")

- **Q4-F3** The dwarfs save her the first time by cutting the laces, after which she begins to breathe again.
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White (Ashliman)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published updated 2005-11 · scope: 1857 edition
  - Quote: "cut the lace in two."
  - Source 2: [Household Tales, tr. Margaret Hunt (Project Gutenberg)](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/5314/5314-h/5314-h.htm) · academic · published undated · scope: final edition
  - Quote 2: "they cut the laces; then she began to breathe a little, and after a while came to life again."
  - Flags: none
  - Check: VERIFIED (Ashliman: "they cut the lace in two" then "she began to breathe a little, and little by little she came back to life." Hunt quote found verbatim.)

- **Q4-F4** The dwarfs then warn Snow White that the peddler was the queen and tell her to let no one in.
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White (Ashliman)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published updated 2005-11 · scope: 1857 edition
  - Quote: "The old peddler woman was no one else but the godless queen. Take care and let no one in when we are not with you."
  - Flags: SINGLE SOURCE
  - Check: VERIFIED (Ashliman; quote found verbatim. WARN: the warning is "when we are not with you", not an absolute ban.)

- **Q4-F5** On the second visit the queen takes the shape of a different old woman and brings a poisoned comb; as soon as it is in Snow White's hair she falls unconscious.
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White (Ashliman)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published updated 2005-11 · scope: 1857 edition
  - Quote: "She had barely stuck the comb into Snow-White's hair when the poison took effect, and the girl fell down unconscious."
  - Source 2: [Household Tales, tr. Margaret Hunt (Project Gutenberg)](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/5314/5314-h/5314-h.htm) · academic · published undated · scope: final edition
  - Quote 2: "took the shape of another old woman"
  - Flags: none [Ashliman also gives "taking the form of a different old woman"]
  - Check: VERIFIED (Ashliman: "taking the form of a different old woman", "a poisoned comb", and the quote found verbatim. Hunt quote also found.)

- **Q4-F6** The dwarfs save her the second time by pulling the comb out of her hair, and she comes to at once.
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White (Ashliman)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published updated 2005-11 · scope: 1857 edition
  - Quote: "They had scarcely pulled it out when Snow-White came to herself again."
  - Source 2: [Household Tales, tr. Margaret Hunt (Project Gutenberg)](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/5314/5314-h/5314-h.htm) · academic · published undated · scope: final edition
  - Quote 2: "Scarcely had they taken it out when Snow-white came to herself"
  - Flags: none
  - Check: VERIFIED (Ashliman; quote found verbatim. "at once" is a paraphrase of "scarcely". Hunt quote also found.)

- **Q4-F7** For the third visit the queen vows Snow White will die, makes a poisoned apple in a secret room, and disguises herself as a peasant (country) woman.
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White (Ashliman)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published updated 2005-11 · scope: 1857 edition
  - Quote: "Snow-White shall die," she shouted, "if it costs me my life!" / "she disguised herself as a peasant woman."
  - Source 2: [Household Tales, tr. Margaret Hunt (Project Gutenberg)](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/5314/5314-h/5314-h.htm) · academic · published undated · scope: final edition
  - Quote 2: "a quite secret, lonely room, where no one ever came" / "dressed herself up as a country-woman"
  - Flags: none
  - Check: VERIFIED (Ashliman: both quotes found; the room is "her most secret room -- no one else was allowed inside". Hunt quotes also found verbatim.)

- **Q4-F8** Only the red half of the apple is poisoned; the queen cuts it in two and eats the white half herself so Snow White will trust it, and after one bite of the red half Snow White falls dead.
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White (Ashliman)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published updated 2005-11 · scope: 1857 edition
  - Quote: "only the red half was poisoned." / "You eat the red half, and I shall eat the white half." / "she fell to the ground dead."
  - Source 2: [Household Tales, tr. Margaret Hunt (Project Gutenberg)](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/5314/5314-h/5314-h.htm) · academic · published undated · scope: final edition
  - Quote 2: "Outside it looked pretty, white with a red cheek" / "you eat the red cheek, and I will eat the white."
  - Flags: none
  - Check: VERIFIED (Hunt fully: "only the red cheek was poisoned", "I will cut the apple in two pieces; you eat the red cheek, and I will eat the white", "when she saw that the woman ate part of it she could resist no longer", "hardly had she a bit of it in her mouth than she fell down dead." Ashliman alone shows the cut and the offer to eat the white half but does not say outright that the woman ate it. WARN: "one bite" paraphrases "barely had a bite in her mouth" / "hardly had she a bit of it in her mouth"; she fell before finishing even one bite.)

- **Q4-F9** After the apple, the dwarfs try the earlier remedies but nothing works; she is revived only later, when the prince's servants stumble while carrying the coffin and the piece of apple is jolted from her throat (final edition). In the 1812 first edition, the same three attempts occur, but the antagonist is her own mother, and a servant hitting her on the back dislodges the apple.
  - Source: [Grimm 053: Little Snow-White (Ashliman)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/grimm053.html) · academic · published updated 2005-11 · scope: 1857 edition
  - Quote: "They undid her laces. They combed her hair... But nothing helped." (cut off)
  - Source 2: [Household Tales, tr. Margaret Hunt (Project Gutenberg)](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/5314/5314-h/5314-h.htm) · academic · published undated · scope: final edition
  - Quote 2: "but it was all of no use; the poor child was dead, and remained dead." / "they stumbled over a tree-stump, and with the shock the poisonous piece of apple" (cut off)
  - Source 3: [Snow-White and other tales of type 709 (Ashliman, includes 1812 text)](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/type0709.html) · academic · published updated 2022-01 · scope: 1812 first edition
  - Quote 3: "They cut the bodice laces in two, and then she could breathe." / "she made a poisoned, poisoned apple." / "He hit her in the back with his hand."
  - Flags: none [EDITION DIFFERENCE: 1812 antagonist is her mother and the apple comes loose from a blow; from 1819 on she is a stepmother and the servant stumbles. The three attempts (laces, comb, apple) are the same in both. Script should follow the 1857 edition consistently. Waking details belong mainly to Q5.]
  - Check: VERIFIED (1857 part: Ashliman "They undid her laces. They combed her hair. They washed her with water and wine. But nothing helped."; Hunt "it was all of no use" and the full stumble sentence "...came out of her throat." 1812 part: type0709 page has "her own mother, not a stepmother", laces, "a poisoned comb", "a poisoned, poisoned apple", and an angry servant who "hit her in the back with his hand". WARN: Ashliman's 1857 translation says "one of them stumbled", while Hunt says "they stumbled"; "the servants stumbled" is supported by Hunt (and the German per Q5-F3). WARN: "the same three attempts" means the same three objects; the 1812 disguises were not checked in detail.)
